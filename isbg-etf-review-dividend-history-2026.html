What Is ISBG?

$ISBG is the IncomeSTKd 1X Bitcoin & 1X Gold Premium ETF, an actively managed fund from Quantify Funds that listed on Cboe on January 20, 2026. It's part of a small lineup with $BTGD and $ISSB.

The pitch is simple: for every $1 you put in, the fund tries to give you about $1 of bitcoin exposure and $1 of gold exposure, so roughly $2 of exposure in total. Quantify calls that return stacking. Then it layers on an options strategy to pay a weekly distribution.

"isbg," "isbg etf" and "isbg dividend history" are some of the searches bringing people to TopDividendETFs.com right now. So here's the honest breakdown, with the real numbers.

How ISBG Works: Return Stacking Plus Options

This is the part most people miss. $ISBG doesn't own bitcoin or gold bars. It doesn't even own big blocks of $IBIT or $GLD. It builds its exposure with options on those two ETFs.

  • The exposure: it buys longer dated call options (3 to 18 months out, per Quantify) on bitcoin and gold. Calls let a small amount of cash control a large position. That's how $1 gets you about $2 of exposure.
  • The income: it sells shorter dated calls and puts (1 to 180 days) to collect option premium. The managers decide day to day how much to sell, based on implied versus realized volatility, skew and the shape of the volatility curve.
  • The rest: most of the money sits in a Treasury money market fund and cash as collateral.

You can see it in the holdings. As of November 12, 2026, Quantify listed about 50% in the First American Government Obligations Fund, about 42% in cash, long $GLD and $IBIT call options at about 10.5% and 6.1% of assets, and short $GLD and $IBIT puts at about -4.4% and -5.2%.

Quantify also says the fund aims to "only distribute what the fund can reasonably expect to generate." Keep that line in mind when you look at the payout trend below.

The Real Test: Total Return

Here's what I actually look at: share price plus every distribution. And on that test, $ISBG has been rough.

  • Since inception: -46.11% at NAV and -46.25% at market price, cumulative, per Quantify's month-end performance table. That's with distributions included.
  • Last 6 months: -35.57% at NAV.
  • Last 3 months: +12.19% at NAV, so it can bounce hard too.
  • Last month: -23.16% at NAV.
  • Price: a 52-week high of $28.49 versus about $10.10 on November 13, 2026, per StockAnalysis.

That one bad month tells the whole story of return stacking. From the September 30 close to the October 30 close, $IBIT fell about 8.4% and $GLD fell about 6.6%, per StockAnalysis prices. With roughly $1 of each per $1 invested, losses on both legs stack on top of each other, and $ISBG dropped 23% at NAV for the month. Leverage cuts both ways.

So when you see a "24% yield" on a data site, remember that number is $2.49 of trailing payouts divided by a share price that has fallen more than 60% from its high. The payouts didn't come close to making up for the price drop. That's what we flag as price decay on our list.

Return of Capital and Taxes

Quantify's page lists return of capital at 95.65%. In plain English: almost all of the recent payout is classified as your own money coming back to you, not income the fund earned in the traditional sense.

Return of capital isn't automatically bad. It's usually not taxed when you receive it, and it lowers your cost basis instead. But when it shows up alongside a falling NAV, it's a warning sign that the fund is handing back principal. Quantify's own disclosure says ROC "may reduce the Fund's NAV and trading price over time." Read the 19a-1 notices on the fund page and check your 1099, and talk to a tax pro about your situation. I wrote more about how ROC works in why NEOS ETFs are tax friendly.

Who ISBG Is (and Isn't) For

  • It might fit a small, speculative slice of a portfolio for someone who is bullish on both bitcoin and gold, wants leveraged exposure to both in one ticker, and can stomach 20%+ monthly swings.
  • It probably doesn't fit anyone who needs reliable income to pay bills. The weekly check has been cut in half in nine months and can keep moving.
  • It's not a safe haven. Gold has a reputation as one, but with leverage and bitcoin stacked on top, this fund behaves nothing like a gold fund.
  • Size matters. At about $5.8 million in assets and a 0.67% median spread, it's tiny and costs more to trade than big ETFs. Use limit orders.

Personally, I'd rather own an income fund that pays 10% and holds its price than chase 20% and watch the principal melt. When I was building toward financial freedom, the funds that did the heavy lifting were the boring ones I could hold through a bad month. The Risks I'd Want You to Know

  • Leverage. About 2x total exposure. Losses in bitcoin and gold add together.
  • Falling payouts. From $0.09 a week in February to $0.0424 in November.
  • Price decay. Down about 46% at NAV since inception, including distributions.
  • Options and derivatives risk. The whole fund is built on options on $IBIT and $GLD. Quantify's own risk section notes losses can be magnified.
  • High cost. 1.14% net is about $114 a year on $10,000, and the fee waiver can end after March 31, 2027.
  • New and tiny. Less than a year of history and under $6 million in assets.

My Takeaway

$ISBG is a clever idea: about $1 of bitcoin plus $1 of gold for every $1 invested, with an active options overlay paying a weekly check. In its first ten months, though, the results have been tough. It paid $2.49 per share across 41 weekly distributions, the payout has been cut by more than half, and the NAV total return since launch is about -46%. The "yield" looks enormous because the price collapsed.

If you buy it, buy it as a small leveraged bet on bitcoin and gold, not as an income fund.
