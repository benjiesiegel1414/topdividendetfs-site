What $NVDY Actually Is

$NVDY is the YieldMax NVDA Option Income Strategy ETF. It launched on May 10, 2023 and it's built around one stock: NVIDIA ($NVDA).

Here's the part most people miss. $NVDY doesn't just own $NVDA shares. It holds mostly Treasury notes, bills and cash, gets its $NVDA exposure through options, and sells call spreads on $NVDA to collect premium. As of November 3, 2026, roughly 95% of the fund sat in Treasuries and cash, with $NVDA options on top. The premium from those options is where the weekly check comes from.

The fund manages about $1.25 billion, charges a 1.09% gross expense ratio, and switched from monthly to weekly payouts in October 2025. It's one of the largest single-stock income ETFs out there. If you want the same breakdown for a much wilder YieldMax fund, I did the $MSTY dividend history last week.

When is the next $NVDY dividend?

If the normal pattern holds, the next $NVDY payout gets declared Wednesday, November 4, 2026, goes ex on Thursday, November 5, and pays Friday, November 6. The amount isn't known until it's declared. Watch Thanksgiving week again: Thursday is the holiday, so the ex-date will likely move.

To get a week's check you need to own $NVDY before the market opens on the ex-date. Buying on the ex-date itself is too late for that week.

What about the older monthly payouts?

Before October 2025, $NVDY paid once a month, so each check was much bigger. YieldMax lists 12 monthly payouts in 2024 totaling about $19.53 per share, including several above $2.40. In 2025 the fund paid about $12.14 per share across 22 payouts as it moved to weekly. Don't line up old and new checks in raw dollars. A monthly check covers about four weeks, and the share price and $NVDA's volatility were very different then. Compare payouts as a percentage of the price at the time.

The NVDY Dividend Yield: Which 30% Number?

YieldMax lists a 30.23% distribution rate as of October 28, 2026. That number takes the newest weekly check, multiplies it by 52 and divides by NAV. Our live list shows about 30% too. The 30-day SEC yield, which only counts net investment income and leaves out option income, was 2.93% as of October 31.

Look backward and you get a different number. The last 52 weekly payouts add up to about $6.13 per share, roughly 55% of today's $11.08 price. That trailing figure is inflated by bigger checks from late 2025, which is exactly why the forward-looking distribution rate is lower.

Here's what I actually look at instead: return of capital. YieldMax estimates that 15 of the 44 payouts in 2026 were 90% or more return of capital, while many others were estimated at 0%. ROC isn't automatically bad, since it lowers your cost basis instead of being taxed right away. But it's a reminder that part of a big yield can be your own money coming back. The 1099 at year end settles the real tax character, so check the 19a-1 notices and your tax forms. I broke down how ROC works in this return of capital explainer.

This is where $NVDY is different from a lot of single-stock income funds. Since launch it's up about 330% at NAV with distributions reinvested, more than triple the S&P 500 over the same stretch, because it rode one of the biggest stock runs in history. That's a real result.

But the recent picture is weaker. Over the last year it returned about 5% at NAV, less than half the S&P 500, and it gave back about 9% in October alone. Selling calls caps the upside, so when $NVDA rallies hard, $NVDY keeps only part of it. When $NVDA drops, the fund takes the hit. Over time that tends to show up as price decay, and our list flags $NVDY for it. Total return is the scoreboard, not yield. If you want that math fund by fund, ETFTotalReturns.com runs it.

The Risks I'd Want You to Know

  • One stock. $NVDY lives and dies with $NVDA. YieldMax's own disclosure: the fund is exposed to all of $NVDA's downside but only part of its upside.
  • Capped upside. Selling calls means big rallies get partly sold away. The fund keeps the losses and gives up some of the gains, which is why the price tends to grind lower over time.
  • Shrinking checks. October 2026 paid about 45% less per week than April. When the price falls or volatility cools, the payout drops with it.
  • Return of capital. A third of the 2026 payouts were estimated to be mostly ROC. Fine for taxes in the short run, but watch your cost basis.
  • Not a paycheck. The amount changes every single week. Never build fixed expenses around it.

My Takeaway

$NVDY pays weekly: declared Wednesday, ex-date Thursday, paid Friday. It has paid about $4.80 per share across 44 checks in 2026, and the latest was $0.0690. The 30% distribution rate is real cash, but the weekly amount has trended down most of the year, and the one-year total return of about 5% trailed the S&P 500 by a wide margin.

The long-term record is the best of the single-stock $NVDA income funds, and that's worth something. But that record came from riding a historic $NVDA run, not from the yield. I'd treat $NVDY as a way to get paid on an $NVDA view, not as the foundation of an income portfolio. If you own it, size it small, decide on purpose whether to reinvest or redeploy the checks, and check total return every quarter. I'd rather build my core on funds with no price decay and keep something like this as a satellite.
