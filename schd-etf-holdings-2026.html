What Stocks Does SCHD Hold in 2026?

The Schwab U.S. Dividend Equity ETF (SCHD) tracks the Dow Jones U.S. Dividend 100 Index, which selects high-quality dividend-paying U.S. stocks screened on free cash flow to total debt, return on equity, dividend yield, and five-year dividend growth rate. As of August 20, 2026, SCHD's holdings file lists 103 positions across 10 sectors, with Schwab publishing the 100 largest by market value.

Abbott Laboratories (ABT) remains the fund's largest holding at 4.86% of assets, worth roughly $5.4 billion. The bigger story is behind it: Merck (MRK) climbed to 4.82% and passed Amgen for the #2 spot, giving SCHD an all-healthcare top three for the first time this year. Amgen (AMGN) sits third at 4.75%.

Energy also pushed higher. Chevron (CVX) at 4.02% and ConocoPhillips (COP) at 4.01% now occupy the #5 and #6 slots — the first time two energy names have sat that high in the book in 2026. ConocoPhillips alone jumped four places in a single week.

SCHD is not heavily concentrated by large-fund standards. No single position exceeds 4.86%, and the top 10 holdings together account for 42.15% of assets — the index enforces a weight cap that prevents any one stock from dominating the portfolio.

What the Sector Mix Tells You

Health Care (21.48%) and Consumer Staples (19.42%) together make up nearly 41% of the fund — a defensive core built around Abbott, Merck, Amgen, UnitedHealth, Coca-Cola, Procter & Gamble, and PepsiCo. This is why SCHD tends to hold up better than the broad market in drawdowns and lag it in momentum-driven rallies. If you want the opposite exposure, our Top Growth ETFs list covers the other end of the spectrum.

Information Technology is only 8.54%, spread across just four names: Texas Instruments, Accenture, Qualcomm, and Skyworks. The sleeve shrank again this week as Texas Instruments fell from 3.32% to 3.19%. SCHD's screens require a meaningful dividend yield plus five years of dividend growth, which structurally excludes most large-cap growth technology. If you own SCHD expecting tech exposure, you don't have it.

Financials has the most names (39) but only 9.74% of weight — mostly small regional banks in the long tail, each under 0.75%. Sector count and sector weight are very different things in this fund, and financials just slipped under 10% for the first time in 2026.

Energy climbed to 15.68%, its highest reading of the year, on continued strength in Chevron, ConocoPhillips, EOG, and Devon. Energy is the sleeve most likely to swing SCHD's weight table between quarterly rebalances. See how energy income funds compare on the Top 100 Dividend ETFs leaderboard.

How SCHD Picks Its Holdings

The rules behind the list — screens, caps, reconstitution, and rebalance dates.

Four Screens, 100 Names, Zero Discretion

SCHD does not pick stocks. It mechanically follows the Dow Jones U.S. Dividend 100 Index, which starts with U.S. companies that have paid dividends for at least 10 consecutive years, then ranks the survivors on four equally weighted metrics: free cash flow to total debt, return on equity, indicated dividend yield, and five-year dividend growth rate.

The top 100 by composite score make the index. Weights are capped so no single stock exceeds 4% at reconstitution and no sector exceeds 25%, which is why you see the top positions clustered tightly between 3.7% and 4.86% rather than one or two names dominating. Note that positions can and do drift above the 4% cap between reconstitutions — six holdings sit above it right now, which is entirely normal.

The index reconstitutes annually each March — that's when names are added and removed. It rebalances quarterly in March, June, September, and December, which only shifts weights among existing holdings. Between those events, weights drift naturally with share prices. The next rebalance lands in September; our Q2 rebalance recap shows what that process looked like last quarter.

⚠️ Why the File Says 103 Positions but Only Shows 100

The Dow Jones U.S. Dividend 100 Index targets exactly 100 stocks. SCHD's disclosed holdings file for 08/20/2026 reports 103 total line items but displays the largest 100 by market value. Those 100 break down as 98 common stocks plus the SSC government money market fund (GVMXX, 0.03%) and a U.S. dollar cash balance (0.02%). The three line items below the display cutoff are the fund's smallest tail positions — in the prior week's file those were Oxford Industries and two index futures contracts (S&P MidCap 400 E-mini and DJIA E-mini, both September 2026) used to equitize uninvested cash between trades. Combined, everything below the cutoff is worth well under 0.05% of assets and has no meaningful effect on returns.

Frequently Asked Questions — SCHD Holdings

Common questions about what SCHD owns, how it's weighted, and how often it changes.

  • What is SCHD's largest holding?+
    As of August 20, 2026, Abbott Laboratories (ABT) is SCHD's largest holding at 4.86% of assets, worth approximately $5.4 billion across 47.1 million shares. Merck (MRK) is a close second at 4.82% after passing Amgen this week, and Amgen (AMGN) is third at 4.75%. All three of the fund's biggest positions are healthcare names. See how that order shifted at the Q2 rebalance →
  • How many stocks does SCHD hold?+
    SCHD's file reports 103 total positions and displays the largest 100 by market value. Those 100 break down to 98 common stocks plus two non-equity line items: the GVMXX government money market fund and a U.S. dollar cash balance. The equity sleeve tracks the Dow Jones U.S. Dividend 100 Index.
  • What are SCHD's top 10 holdings?+
    SCHD's top 10 as of August 20, 2026: Abbott 4.86%, Merck 4.82%, Amgen 4.75%, Coca-Cola 4.26%, Chevron 4.02%, ConocoPhillips 4.01%, Home Depot 3.96%, Verizon 3.91%, UnitedHealth 3.78%, and Procter & Gamble 3.78%. Together they represent 42.15% of the fund. PepsiCo remains just outside at #11 with 3.70%.
  • What sectors does SCHD invest in?+
    SCHD's largest sector is Health Care at 21.48%, then Consumer Staples 19.42%, Energy 15.68%, Industrials 11.71%, and Financials 9.74%. Information Technology is 8.54%, Consumer Discretionary 6.97%, Communication Services 6.34%, with small Utilities and cash allocations. Financials dipped below 10% this week for the first time in 2026.
  • Does SCHD hold any technology stocks?+
    Yes, but only 8.54% across four names: Texas Instruments 3.19%, Accenture 2.72%, Qualcomm 2.38%, and Skyworks Solutions 0.25%. SCHD screens for dividend yield plus five years of dividend growth, which structurally excludes most large-cap growth technology. If you hold SCHD for tech exposure, you don't have it — our Top Growth ETFs list is where that exposure actually lives.
  • Does SCHD hold Apple, Microsoft, or Nvidia?+
    No. None of the three are in SCHD. Apple and Microsoft pay dividends but their yields are far too low to clear the index's yield screen, and Nvidia's yield is negligible. SCHD ranks candidates on indicated dividend yield as one of four equally weighted factors, so mega-cap low-yielders rarely score highly enough to make the top 100.
  • How concentrated is SCHD?+
    Moderately. No single holding exceeds 4.86%, and the top 10 account for 42.15% of assets. The index caps individual weights at 4% and sector weights at 25% at each reconstitution, which prevents runaway concentration — though drift between rebalances currently has six names sitting above that 4% line. For comparison, the top 10 of the S&P 500 regularly exceed 35% with far higher single-name weights.
  • How often does SCHD update its holdings?+
    Schwab publishes SCHD's full holdings file daily after market close. The index itself reconstitutes annually in March (adding and removing names) and rebalances quarterly in March, June, September, and December (adjusting weights only). This page is refreshed against Schwab's official file. PRO members get the daily snapshot archive →
  • Why do SCHD's holdings weights keep changing?+
    Two reasons. First, weights drift continuously with share prices — Merck gained 0.40 points and moved from #3 to #2 in five sessions without any change in the fund's strategy. Second, the quarterly rebalance resets weights back toward index targets. Neither indicates active management; SCHD is fully rules-based.
  • Why did the futures contracts disappear from the list?+
    They didn't — they simply fell below Schwab's display cutoff. The file reports 103 total positions but only publishes the largest 100 by market value. Index funds hold small futures positions to equitize uninvested cash between trades, and those contracts carry a market value close to zero, which puts them at the very bottom of any list sorted by size.
