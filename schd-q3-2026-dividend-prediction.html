SCHD's Q3 2026 distribution is expected to go ex-dividend on September 23 and pay on September 28. Between now and then, the only thing anyone has is a model. So we built one, showed all of the work, and then put a prediction tool at the bottom of this page so you can put your own number on the board.

Here is the short version. Fifteen SCHD holdings raised their dividends this quarter, two cut, and Ethan Allen ($ETD) dropped a $3.00 per share special dividend. Every one of those is a real input. But when you actually weight them by position size, they add up to a much smaller number than the headlines suggest, and the thing that will most likely decide this quarter is not raises at all. It is share count.

Method 1: Weight the raises properly

This is the method most people skip, and it is the one that changes the most minds. A dividend raise only matters to SCHD in proportion to how much of the fund that company represents. Multiply each raising holding's weight by the size of its raise, sum across all fifteen, and you get the actual incremental income contribution.

⚠️ Read that bottom row again

Fifteen raises, headline increases as large as 13.2%, and the whole wave nets out to about +0.42% of fund income after the $SWKS suspension. That is not a rounding error, but it is nowhere near enough to move a quarterly payout from $0.2525 to $0.2840. Anyone telling you the raises alone justify a 12% jump has not done this multiplication.

The reason is simple arithmetic. $BX at a 2.74% weight raising 11.2% contributes more than the other fourteen raises combined. And $SWKS, a single 0.26% position, wipes out more than a third of the entire raise wave by going to zero. Small positions raising 4.7% feel meaningful when you read the headline. They are worth about two thousandths of a percent each.

Method 2: Normalize the Q2 share creation drag

This is where the real story is. SCHD paid $0.2525 in Q2 2026 against $0.2602 in Q2 2025, a decline of 3.0% at a time when the underlying holdings were collectively growing their dividends. That combination should not happen in a healthy quarter.

Schwab's explanation was share creation timing. A large wave of new SCHD shares was created late in Q2, after many of the underlying holdings had already gone ex-dividend. Those new shares participated in the distribution but had not been present to collect the income behind it. The result is dilution of the per-share payout that has nothing to do with the health of the portfolio.

We can size that drag. If holdings were organically growing dividends around 4% year over year, Q2 2026 should have come in near $0.2706. It came in at $0.2525. That gap implies a drag of roughly 6.7% in that single quarter.

Apply that same 4% organic growth to Q3 2025's $0.2604 and you get $0.2708 for a quarter with no creation drag. Add the +0.42% net raise effect and the $0.0005 from $ETD's special and you land around $0.2722.

This is also the honest explanation for why a big quarter-over-quarter jump is plausible at all. The $0.2525 base is artificially low. Comparing Q3 to a depressed Q2 will produce a percentage that looks dramatic without requiring anything dramatic to happen.

🏆 The whole disagreement is one binary question

SCHD's largest quarterly distribution ever is $0.2782, paid in Q4 2025. At $0.2840 the tracker's estimate clears that and sets a record. At $0.2735 ours does not, by about half a cent. Every other difference between the two is noise. This one is a yes or no.

Here is the case for taking the record seriously. Q3 2026 has an unusually strong setup: fifteen raises across six sectors, a clean quarter with no repeat of the Q2 creation drag, and a base quarter that was artificially depressed. If the drag fully reverses and the raises land inside the collection window, $0.2840 is reachable.

Here is the case against it. SCHD has never posted its annual high in Q3. Q4 was the largest quarter in both 2024 and 2025, and the pattern is structural rather than coincidental, because a number of large holdings concentrate payments late in the year. Asking Q3 2026 to break a record set by a Q4 means asking it to break seasonality at the same time. And the same inflows that caused the Q2 problem have not stopped.

What would push the number higher

  • Full reversal of the Q2 creation drag. If those shares are now fully seasoned and collecting, the per-share snapback is larger than our 4% organic assumption allows.
  • $MO announces its annual raise in time. Altria is a top-10 SCHD position and typically announces in August. A raise that lands inside the Q3 collection window is worth meaningfully more than any of the fifteen already counted.
  • $BX runs hotter than its declared $1.29. Blackstone's variable dividend is the largest single lever in the fund at a 2.74% weight.
  • September rebalance adds higher-yielding names in time for their ex-dates to fall inside the quarter.

What would push it lower

  • Inflows keep arriving late. This is the single biggest risk and it is the same one that produced the Q2 miss. SCHD's asset growth has been persistent, not a one-quarter event.
  • $SWKS stays in the portfolio through the quarter. A 0.26% position paying nothing is a real drag on collected income whether or not it survives the September rebalance.
  • Collection timing pushes dividends into Q4. Several of the fifteen raises may not have an ex-date inside SCHD's Q3 window at all, in which case they show up next quarter instead.
  • Seasonality holds. Q3 has simply never been SCHD's biggest quarter.

Q3 2026 SCHD Prediction FAQ

What is the predicted SCHD dividend for Q3 2026?

Our model estimates $0.2735 per share, with a reasonable range of $0.2650 to $0.2825. That would be roughly 5.0% higher than Q3 2025's $0.2604 and about 8.3% higher than Q2 2026's $0.2525. Our Q3 dividend tracker carries a separate, more optimistic estimate of $0.2840. Neither is something to trade on. Schwab sets the actual amount and typically declares in the second half of September.

When is SCHD's Q3 2026 ex-dividend date and pay date?

The expected ex-dividend date is September 23, 2026, with an expected pay date of September 28, 2026. You need to own shares before the ex-dividend date to receive the distribution. Both dates are expectations based on SCHD's normal schedule and are only confirmed when Schwab declares.

Would $0.2840 be SCHD's highest quarterly dividend ever?

Yes. SCHD's largest split-adjusted quarterly distribution to date is $0.2782, paid in Q4 2025. An estimate of $0.2840 clears that and sets a record. Our $0.2735 estimate does not. That single question, record or no record, is the only place the two estimates meaningfully disagree.

How much do the 15 holding dividend raises actually add?

Less than most people expect. Multiplying each raising holding's weight in SCHD by the size of its raise and summing across all fifteen gives about +0.70% of incremental fund income. Subtracting the drag from $SWKS's suspension and $WEN's 50% cut leaves a net of roughly +0.42%. The raises are a real positive, but they are not what drives a large quarter-over-quarter move.

Why was SCHD's Q2 2026 dividend lower than Q2 2025?

Q2 2026 paid $0.2525 against Q2 2025's $0.2602, a decline of 3.0%. Schwab pointed to a surge in SCHD share creation late in the quarter, after many underlying holdings had already gone ex-dividend. Those newly created shares could not collect dividends that had already gone ex, which diluted the per-share distribution. Backing out normal holding-level growth implies a drag of roughly 6% to 7% in that quarter.

How much does Ethan Allen's $3.00 special dividend add to SCHD?

Roughly $0.0005 per SCHD share, or about a twentieth of a cent. $ETD is SCHD's smallest equity position at approximately 0.01% of the fund. The $3.00 per share special is genuinely large relative to Ethan Allen's own history, at 12 times its last special, but a 0.01% position is still a 0.01% position. It is worth about 0.2% of a typical quarterly distribution.

Could Altria ($MO) still change the picture?

Yes, and it is the largest remaining unknown on the raise side. $MO is a top-10 SCHD holding and typically announces its annual dividend increase in August. Because of its weight, a single $MO raise carries more income impact than most of the fifteen already tracked. Whether it lands inside SCHD's Q3 collection window is a separate question from whether it is announced.
