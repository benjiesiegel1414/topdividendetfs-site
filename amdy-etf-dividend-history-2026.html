What $AMDY Actually Is

$AMDY is the YieldMax AMD Option Income Strategy ETF. It launched on September 18, 2023 and it's built around one stock: Advanced Micro Devices ($AMD).

Here's the part most people miss. $AMDY doesn't simply own $AMD shares. Its biggest holdings are U.S. Treasury notes and bills plus cash, it gets its $AMD exposure through options, and it sells call spreads on $AMD to collect premium. YieldMax describes the goal as harvesting option premium from $AMD's volatility while still participating in some of its upside. That premium is where the weekly check comes from.

The fund manages about $339 million (November 20, 2026), charges a 1.00% gross expense ratio, and switched from monthly to weekly payouts in October 2025. If you want the same breakdown for YieldMax's NVIDIA fund, I did the $NVDY dividend history earlier this month.

When is the next $AMDY dividend?

Next week is Thanksgiving week, and Thursday, November 26 is a market holiday, so the usual Thursday ex-date can't happen. Last year YieldMax declared the Thanksgiving payout on Wednesday, set the ex-date for Friday, and paid the following Monday. If it follows that pattern again, expect an ex-date around Friday, November 27, 2026 and payment around Monday, November 30. That's my read of the pattern, not an announcement, and the amount isn't known until it's declared.

To get a week's check you need to own $AMDY before the market opens on the ex-date. Buying on the ex-date itself is too late for that week.

What about the older monthly payouts?

Before late October 2025, $AMDY paid once a month, so each check was bigger. YieldMax lists 12 monthly payouts in 2024 totaling about $11.12 per share. In 2025 the fund paid about $7.05 per share across 21 payouts: 11 monthly checks through October 16 (about $4.78) and then 10 weekly checks. Don't line up old and new checks in raw dollars. A monthly check covers about four weeks, and the share price and $AMD's volatility were very different then. Compare payouts as a percentage of the price at the time.

The AMDY Dividend Yield: Which 56% Number?

YieldMax lists a 55.61% distribution rate as of November 18, 2026. That number takes the newest weekly check, multiplies it by 52 and divides by NAV. Our live list shows about 56% too. The 30-day SEC yield, which only counts net investment income and leaves out option income, was 2.81% as of October 31.

Look backward and you get a much bigger number. The last 52 weekly payouts add up to about $29.96 per share, roughly 90% of today's $33.36 price. That trailing figure is inflated by the giant checks from April through July, which is exactly why the forward-looking distribution rate is lower.

Here's what I actually look at instead: return of capital. YieldMax estimates that 19 of the 47 payouts in 2026 were 90% or more return of capital, while 12 were estimated at 0%. The latest check was estimated at about 95% ROC. ROC isn't automatically bad, since it lowers your cost basis instead of being taxed right away. But it's a reminder that part of a big yield can be your own money coming back. The 1099 at year end settles the real tax character, so check the 19a-1 notices and your tax forms. I broke down how ROC works in this return of capital explainer.

The Risks I'd Want You to Know

  • One stock. $AMDY lives and dies with $AMD. YieldMax's own disclosure: the fund captures only a portion of $AMD's gains and is subject to all of its potential losses, which the option premium may not offset.
  • Capped upside. Selling calls means big rallies get partly sold away. It worked out this year, but in a sharp drop the fund keeps the losses.
  • Swinging checks. The weekly payout ran from $0.28 to $1.17 in 2026. When $AMD's price falls or its volatility cools, the payout drops with it.
  • Return of capital. About 40% of the 2026 payouts were estimated to be mostly ROC. Fine for taxes in the short run, but watch your cost basis.
  • Not a paycheck. The amount changes every single week. Never build fixed expenses around it.

My Takeaway

$AMDY pays weekly: declared Wednesday, ex-date Thursday, paid Friday, with Thanksgiving week likely shifting the next one. It has paid about $28.55 per share across 47 checks in 2026, and the latest was $0.4258. The 56% distribution rate is real cash, and this year the total return was excellent, about 96% through October at NAV.

But that result came from riding a huge $AMD run, not from the yield, and October showed how fast it can reverse. I'd treat $AMDY as a way to get paid on an $AMD view, not as the foundation of an income portfolio. If you own it, size it small, decide on purpose whether to reinvest or redeploy the checks, and check total return every quarter. I'd rather build my core on funds with no price decay and keep something like this as a satellite.
