Twelve SCHD Holdings Have Raised Dividends This Quarter — Big or Small, We Report Them All

If you're an SCHD dividend investor waiting to see what the Schwab U.S. Dividend Equity ETF pays in Q3 2026, the raises keep stacking up. Because SCHD is a pass-through vehicle — it collects the dividends paid by its ~100 underlying holdings and distributes them to shareholders — every dividend increase announced by an SCHD holding flows directly into SCHD's future payouts.

The newest name on the board is Broadridge Financial Solutions ($BR), which just announced an 11.8% dividend increase. $BR was one of the additions at SCHD's 2026 index reconstitution and currently sits at roughly a 0.49% weight in the fund. That makes it a mid-tier position — not a needle-mover on its own, but $BR is exactly the kind of high-quality, consistent dividend grower the Dow Jones U.S. Dividend 100 methodology is built to capture.

The largest percentage raise this quarter is still Regions Financial ($RF) at +13.2%, with $BR now slotting in second and Blackstone ($BX) third at +11.2% (variable — more on that below). All told, twelve SCHD holdings have raised across financials, industrials, consumer discretionary, consumer staples, and energy. A thirteenth, Ethan Allen Interiors ($ETD), held its regular dividend flat but declared a $0.25 per share special dividend — cash that still lands in SCHD's collection bucket even though it isn't a rate increase.

🆕 Spotlight: Broadridge Financial Solutions ($BR) +11.8%

Broadridge is the back-office plumbing of Wall Street — proxy processing, shareholder communications, and trade settlement technology used by brokerages and asset managers. It's a fee-based, recurring-revenue business, which is why it screens well on SCHD's free cash flow and return-on-equity filters. $BR joined the fund at the 2026 reconstitution and now carries roughly a 0.49% weight. An 11.8% raise from a name that size won't single-handedly lift SCHD's payout, but it's the second-largest percentage increase of the quarter and a strong signal about the underlying business.

💰 Ethan Allen ($ETD): No Raise, But a $0.25 Special Dividend

$ETD kept its regular quarterly dividend unchanged this period but declared a $0.25 per share special dividend. Special dividends are one-time payments — they don't raise the ongoing dividend rate and shouldn't be counted toward a company's growth streak. That said, SCHD collects the cash all the same, so a special can create a small one-quarter bump in a fund's distribution that doesn't repeat. It's worth tracking, but worth labeling correctly.

📌 A Note on Blackstone's ($BX) Variable Dividend

Blackstone pays a variable dividend tied to its distributable earnings, not a fixed quarterly rate. The move from $1.16 to $1.29 per share (+11.2%) reflects stronger earnings this period — but unlike a traditional dividend raise from a company like $FAST or $TGT, it can move down in a weaker quarter. Treat variable-dividend payers differently from committed dividend growers when you're modeling income.

✅ Why Holding Raises Matter for SCHD Investors

SCHD doesn't set its own dividend — its quarterly payout is determined by the actual cash dividends collected from its holdings during the period. When names like Regions Financial raise 13.2% and Broadridge raises 11.8%, SCHD collects more income per share it holds, which supports the fund's own distribution over time. The twelve holdings raising this quarter span financials ($RF, $BX, $PFG, $OZK, $SRCE, $CPF), industrials ($FAST, $BR), consumer discretionary ($DRI, $PAG), consumer staples ($TGT), and energy ($DINO) — a broad, multi-sector wave and a healthy signal for SCHD's income stream heading into the back half of 2026.

Financials Are Leading This Quarter's Raises

Look at the sector breakdown and one thing jumps out: six of the twelve raises came from financials — $RF, $BX, $PFG, $OZK, $SRCE, and $CPF. Regional banks in particular ($RF, $OZK, $SRCE, $CPF) are stepping up payouts, which historically follows a period of improved capital positions and stress-test clearance.

That matters for SCHD specifically because financials are one of SCHD's larger sector weights. When a sector that carries meaningful weight in the index raises broadly rather than in isolation, the effect on the fund's collected income is more pronounced than a single large-cap raise elsewhere in the portfolio. And with $BR now joining $FAST on the industrials side, the increases are showing up in more than one corner of the portfolio.

What Could This Mean for SCHD's Q3 2026 Payout?

SCHD's Q3 distribution is typically declared and paid in late September. While no one knows the exact amount until Schwab announces it, here's the historical context that matters:

Last year, SCHD paid $0.2604 in Q3 2025 — up from $0.2515 in Q3 2024 (split-adjusted). SCHD's Q3 payouts have historically come in near or slightly above Q2 levels, and with twelve holdings actively raising their dividends right now, the ingredients for continued year-over-year dividend growth are in place. The Q2 2026 payout of $0.2525 was modestly softer than Q2 2025's $0.2602 — consistent with SCHD's normal quarter-to-quarter variability — so income investors will be watching Q3 closely to gauge the full-year trajectory.

⚠️ Important: Raises Don't Guarantee a Bigger SCHD Dividend

SCHD's payout also depends on index reconstitution, sector weightings, share creation/redemption activity, and the timing of when holding dividends are actually collected. Twelve raises inside the fund is a positive signal, not a promise. Always wait for the official declaration from Schwab Asset Management.

The Bigger Picture: Why Dividend Growth Investors Watch Holding Announcements

SCHD's index (the Dow Jones U.S. Dividend 100) screens for companies with at least 10 consecutive years of dividend payments, strong free cash flow, healthy return on equity, and dividend growth. That's why quarters like this one — where twelve holdings across five sectors announce raises within weeks of each other — are the engine behind SCHD's decade-plus streak of rising annual payouts. Target's raise, while modest at 1.8%, is a textbook SCHD holding move: another year added to one of the longest consecutive dividend increase streaks in the market.

The smaller names on this list are worth watching for a different reason. $SRCE and $CPF are the kind of small regional banks that rarely make headlines but quietly compound their payouts year after year. $PAG's 1.4% bump continues a steady post-2020 raise cadence. $BR is a newer addition proving its dividend growth credentials immediately. And $BX shows the trade-off of variable dividends: bigger swings up, but no floor underneath them.

For income investors, tracking these announcements gives you an early read on the fund's distribution trajectory before Schwab's official declaration hits. It's one of the simplest ways to stay ahead of the quarterly payout — and it's exactly the kind of monitoring we automate on our Pro platform.
