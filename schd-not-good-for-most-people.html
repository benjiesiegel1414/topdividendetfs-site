$SCHD Is NOT a Good ETF for Most People (Unless You Have One of These 2 Things)

I know. Calm down. Hear me out. $SCHD is one of the best dividend ETFs ever built, and it's still the wrong fund for a lot of the people piling into it. Here's the math nobody shows you.

The Sentence That Stuck With Me

I was talking with another dividend investor this week, and he said something I couldn't shake:

"$SCHD is a great ETF. It's just not a great ETF for most of the people buying it."

The more I thought about it, the more I agreed. And no, this isn't a hit piece. I've been a dividend investor for over 10 years, and $SCHD is about as clean as dividend ETFs get. The problem isn't the fund. The problem is the mismatch between what $SCHD does and what most new buyers expect it to do.

Most people find $SCHD because someone online called it "the king of dividend ETFs." Then they put $5,000 in, check their first quarterly payout, and wonder why it bought them lunch.

Thing #1: A LOT of Money

At a ~3.2% yield, here's what $SCHD actually pays you before taxes.

Want $1,000 a month from $SCHD? You'd need about $375,000. That's $12,000 a year divided by a 3.2% yield. Most people buying their first shares of $SCHD are nowhere near that number.

If you're starting with $5,000 or $10,000 and hoping for real income soon, $SCHD is going to feel painfully slow. A $10,000 position pays about $80 a quarter. That's not a paycheck, that's a nice dinner.

None of that is a knock on $SCHD. It's just math. Low yield times a small balance equals a small check. You can run your own numbers with our free $SCHD dividend calculator, or project it years out on DividendProjection.com.

Thing #2: A LOT of Time

This is where $SCHD shines. Its +593% total return since 2011 works out to roughly 13.7% a year.

If that pace held (and there's no guarantee it will), here's what a one-time $10,000 investment with dividends reinvested could grow into:

That's the magic of $SCHD. Thirty years of compounding turns $10,000 into a position that pays about $15,000 a year. But look at the 5-year row. Over 5 years, it's just "fine." The magic needs decades to work, because a lot of $SCHD's return comes from the price going up and the dividend growing, not from a big check today.

That 13.7% figure is a backward-looking average from a strong stretch for U.S. stocks. Future returns could be lower, and there will be ugly years along the way. Hypothetical example for illustration only.

The Real Problem: Small Account + Need Income Soon

Put those two together and you see who gets burned. If you have a small portfolio and you need income in the next few years, $SCHD is the wrong tool for that specific job. Compare what $50,000 pays in $SCHD versus a few higher yield ETFs that have shown no price decay so far:

That's a big gap. But before anyone sells everything for a 14% yield, here's the honest part:

  • Different strategies. $SPYI, $QQQI and $GPIX sell options to generate that income. In a big rally they give up some of the upside that $SCHD's holdings keep.
  • Much shorter track records. $SPYI launched in August 2022 (+78% total return), $GPIX in October 2023 (+80%) and $QQQI in January 2024 (+55%). Those are good numbers, but they come from a few mostly strong years, not 15 years with every kind of market.
  • The income isn't fixed. Option income moves with market volatility, and the monthly checks change. Some distributions can also include return of capital.
  • Taxes work differently. $SCHD pays mostly qualified dividends. $SPYI and $QQQI use index options that get Section 1256 tax treatment. Talk to a tax pro about your situation.

My Takeaway

$SCHD isn't bad. It's one of the best dividend ETFs ever made: a 0.06% fee, no price decay, and +593% since 2011. But it's a time machine, not a paycheck. If you have decades, or you already have a big pile of money, it's hard to beat. If you have $10,000 and need income next year, it's going to feel like watching paint dry.

Frequently Asked Questions: Is SCHD a Good ETF?

  • Is SCHD a good ETF for beginners?+
    It can be, if you have a long time horizon. $SCHD has a 0.06% expense ratio, about $120.6 billion in assets and a +593% total return since its October 2011 launch. But at a yield of about 3.2%, a small account will produce very little income for years, so beginners who need income soon may be disappointed.
  • How much do I need in SCHD to make $1,000 a month?+
    About $375,000. $1,000 a month is $12,000 a year, and $12,000 divided by a 3.2% yield is $375,000. Yields change, so the exact amount moves with the share price and the dividend.
  • How much does $10,000 in SCHD pay?+
    At a yield of about 3.2%, $10,000 in $SCHD pays roughly $320 a year, or about $27 a month, before taxes. $SCHD pays quarterly, so that comes in four checks of about $80.
  • Is SCHD better than SPYI or QQQI?+
    They do different jobs. $SCHD is a dividend growth fund with a low yield and a long track record. $SPYI and $QQQI are option income funds that pay much more today (about 12.2% and 14.3%) with no price decay so far, but they give up some upside when markets rip and have much shorter histories. Many investors use both.
  • Why is SCHD's yield so low?+
    $SCHD owns established companies that pay and grow dividends, not the highest yielders. Its yield has stayed in the 3% to 4% range, and the payout grows over time as its holdings raise dividends. The trade-off is less income today for more growth later.
