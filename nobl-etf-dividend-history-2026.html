What $NOBL Actually Is

$NOBL is the ProShares S&P 500 Dividend Aristocrats ETF. It launched on October 9, 2013, and ProShares calls it the only ETF focused exclusively on the S&P 500 Dividend Aristocrats: S&P 500 companies that have raised their dividend for at least 25 years in a row.

Right now that's about 69 to 70 companies. The index equal weights them, so no single stock dominates. As of November 6, 2026, the biggest position (Pentair) was only 1.82% of the fund, and names like Stanley Black & Decker, Roper, Clorox and Lowe's all sat around 1.6% to 1.7%. It manages about $12.1 billion and charges 0.35% a year.

Here's the part most people miss: $NOBL is not a high yield fund. You're buying a club of companies with long dividend raising streaks, not the biggest checks. That's why the yield is around 2%. The appeal is the growth and the quality, not the starting income.

When is the next $NOBL dividend?

ProShares hasn't declared the December 2026 payout yet. But the pattern is very consistent: the last four December ex-dates were December 20 to 24, and the cash landed by December 31 every time. So expect the next ex-date in the week before Christmas. You need to own shares before the ex-date to get it. Check ProShares' distributions page for the exact date once it's announced.

The Pattern Most People Miss: December Is the Big Check

Look at the table again. In every year from 2022 to 2025, the December dividend was the largest of the year, and the March dividend was the smallest. In 2025 it went $0.233, $0.275, $0.274, then $0.331.

That's just how the underlying companies pay. Plenty of Aristocrats announce their raises late in the year, and the timing of when each company's dividend lands in the fund's quarter isn't even. So don't panic when the March check looks light. Compare each quarter to the same quarter a year earlier.

Do that for 2026 and it looks healthy. March was up 10.1% versus March 2025, June was up 10.4%, and September was up 3.0%. Through three quarters, $NOBL has paid 7.7% more than the same stretch of 2025.

The share price has held up while the dividend grew, which is the combination I want from a dividend growth fund.

The Risks I'd Want You to Know

  • Low starting income. About 2% means $NOBL is a slow builder, not a paycheck replacement on its own.
  • Higher fee than the alternatives. 0.35% versus 0.04% to 0.06% for $VIG and $SCHD adds up over decades.
  • Lags in tech led markets. Very few mega cap tech stocks qualify, so $NOBL can trail the S&P 500 for long stretches, like the last 5 years.
  • A streak can end. If a company cuts or freezes its dividend, it gets dropped from the index, often after the price has already fallen.
  • Uneven quarterly checks. The December payout is usually the biggest and March the smallest, so don't budget off one quarter.

My Takeaway

$NOBL has paid a dividend every quarter, and the payout has grown from $0.87 per share in 2022 to $1.11 in 2025, with 2026 running about 7.7% ahead of last year through September. The latest check was $0.28253, the trailing yield is about 2%, and the December payout, usually the biggest of the year, should go ex in the week before Christmas.

I like $NOBL for what it is: a simple, equal weighted way to own the companies with the longest dividend raising streaks in the S&P 500, with no price decay. What I don't like is paying 0.35% for it when $SCHD and $VIG cost a fraction of that. If the 25 year rule and equal weighting matter to you, it's a quality core holding. If you mostly want income, $SCHD pays more for less. Either way, reinvest early and let the growth do the work.
