What $ULTY Actually Is

$ULTY is the YieldMax Ultra Option Income Strategy ETF. It launched on February 28, 2024, and unlike most YieldMax funds it isn't tied to one stock. It holds a basket of 15 to 30 stocks, picked mainly for high implied volatility, and sells call spreads on them to collect premium. That premium is what pays the weekly check.

Here's what's inside right now. YieldMax's top 10 holdings as of November 16, 2026 were Analog Devices (5.70%), Alphabet (5.28%), SpaceX (4.91%), Shopify (4.69%), the VanEck Gold Miners ETF (4.56%), AMD (4.50%), Alibaba (4.45%), Lam Research (4.18%), MercadoLibre (4.18%) and Take-Two Interactive (4.03%). That's a lot more spread out than a single-stock fund like $MSTY, and the manager rotates the names regularly.

The fund manages about $526 million and charges a 1.40% gross expense ratio, which is high even by YieldMax standards. It paid monthly at launch, switched to weekly payouts in March 2025, and did a 1-for-10 reverse split on December 1, 2025. That split matters a lot when you read the payout history, so I'll come back to it.

When is the next $ULTY dividend?

If the normal pattern holds, the next $ULTY payout gets declared Tuesday, November 17, 2026, goes ex on Wednesday, November 18, and pays Thursday, November 19. The amount isn't known until it's declared. Thanksgiving week is next after that: Thursday is the holiday, so expect the pay date to move, like it did last year.

To get a week's check you need to own $ULTY before the market opens on the ex-date. Buying on the ex-date itself is too late for that week.

Why the old payouts look tiny: the 1-for-10 reverse split

If you pull up older $ULTY data you'll see weekly checks of $0.08 to $0.12 in 2025, then a sudden jump to $0.5881 in December. The fund didn't suddenly get generous. $ULTY did a 1-for-10 reverse split effective December 1, 2025, so every 10 old shares became 1 new share. Multiply the old checks by 10 to compare. The last weekly payout before the split was $0.0594 (ex-date November 26, 2025), which is about $0.59 on today's share count.

On that split-adjusted basis, $ULTY paid about $100.19 per share across 10 monthly checks in 2024 and about $53.38 across 46 payouts in 2025, from a launch price of about $193.50 adjusted ($19.35 before the split). Since launch it has paid about $170.28 per share, split-adjusted. Reverse splits usually happen after a share price has fallen a long way, and this one was no exception.

The ULTY Dividend Yield: Which 60% Number?

YieldMax lists a 60.04% distribution rate as of November 10, 2026. That takes the newest weekly check, multiplies it by 52 and divides by NAV. Our live list shows about 60% too. The 30-day SEC yield, which only counts net investment income and leaves out option income, was -1.71% as of October 31.

Now look backward. The last 52 weekly payouts add up to about $20.63 per share split-adjusted. That's roughly 100% of today's $20.48 price. Read that again: over the past year, $ULTY paid out about as much cash as one share is now worth. That's not a sign the fund is printing money. It's a sign the share price has fallen to meet the payout.

Here's the part most people miss: return of capital. YieldMax estimates that 34 of the 45 payouts in 2026 were 90% or more return of capital, including the latest one at 100%. ROC isn't automatically bad, since it lowers your cost basis instead of being taxed right away. But when a fund's price is falling and most of the checks are classified as ROC, a big part of that 60% is your own money coming back. The 1099 at year end settles the real tax character, so check the 19a-1 notices. I broke down how ROC works in this return of capital explainer.

The Risks I'd Want You to Know

  • Price decay. The share price fell from about $40 right after the reverse split to $20.48. Funds that pay out more than they earn tend to shrink, and the payout shrinks with them.
  • Shrinking checks. The latest check is about 59% smaller than the first one after the split. There's no floor.
  • Capped upside, full downside. Selling call spreads means the fund gives up a chunk of the big up days on its holdings but still takes the losses.
  • High fees. A 1.40% gross expense ratio comes out of your return every year.
  • Mostly return of capital. 34 of 45 payouts in 2026 were estimated at 90% ROC or more. Track your cost basis.
  • Not a paycheck. The amount changes every single week. Never build fixed expenses around it.

My Takeaway

$ULTY pays weekly: declared Tuesday, ex-date Wednesday, paid Thursday. It has paid about $16.71 per share across 45 checks in 2026, and the latest was $0.2425, the smallest since the reverse split. The 60% distribution rate is real cash, but over the past year the cash and the price drop have roughly cancelled each other out.

I've been investing for dividends for over 10 years, and the lesson I keep coming back to is simple: income that eats its own principal isn't really income. I'd rather own something like $CHPY or $JEPQ and collect a smaller check that doesn't eat into my principal. If you own $ULTY, size it small, reinvest at least part of the checks if you want to keep your position from melting, and check total return every quarter, not the yield.
