Why this list exists

What “No Price Decay” Means, and Why It Matters

A 20% yield sounds amazing until the share price falls 30%. Price decay is the slow erosion of a fund's share price, often because it pays out more than it earns.

📉

What price decay is

The fund's share price drifts lower over time. You collect income, but your principal shrinks, so the “yield” is partly your own money coming back.

🛡️

Why no-decay matters

If the price is still above launch, the income has not come at the cost of your principal. $10,000 stays $10,000 or more while it pays you.

🧮

Yield vs. total return

Yield is just the payout. Total return adds the price change. A high yield with a weak total return is a warning sign, so we show both.

FAQ

Common Questions

What is the highest-yielding ETF with no price decay?

As of November 10, 2026, AMDW (AMD WeeklyPay ETF) has the highest yield among ETFs with no price decay at 60.8%, followed by CHPY at 40% and AHD at 34%. The list updates daily.

What does no price decay mean for an ETF?

It means the fund's current share price is at or above the price it launched at. The fund has paid its distributions without its share price falling below where it started.

Which ETFs are on the top 15 list?

The top five right now are AMDW (60.8%), CHPY (40%), AHD (34%), XLEI (20.9%), AMZP (17.4%). The full ranked list of 15 is on this page and refreshes automatically from live data.

Is a high yield with no price decay safe?

Not automatically. No price decay describes past price behavior only. Many of these funds use options strategies that cap upside, some are newer and have not been through a bear market, and yields can change quickly. Always check the prospectus and total return.

What is the difference between yield and total return?

Yield is the income a fund pays, shown as a percentage of its price. Total return adds the change in share price. A fund with a high yield and a falling price can still lose money, which is why we show total return next to every yield.

How often is this list updated?

The rankings pull from the TopDividendETFs PRO database every time the page loads, so yields, total returns and the order of the list stay current.
