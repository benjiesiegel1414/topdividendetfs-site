Why $DIVO Is Different From Most Income ETFs

Most covered call funds are built to throw off a huge yield. $DIVO isn't. It owns about 20 to 25 high-quality, dividend-growing large caps (think $MSFT, $AMGN, $V, $AAPL, $GS and $JPM), and only sells covered calls tactically, on individual stocks, when the managers think the premium is worth it.

The result is a lower yield, around 4.9%, but a fund that has actually grown its price and its payout. It's been around since December 2016, now manages about $8.0 billion, and charges 0.56% a year.

This page is the calendar: when $DIVO goes ex-dividend, when the cash hits your account, and how much every payment was. If you're comparing it with the higher-yield S&P 500 option funds, I broke down the same calendar for $GPIX 2026 dividend dates and $JEPI 2026 dividend dates.

How to read the $DIVO schedule

$DIVO goes ex near the end of the month, usually one business day before month-end, and pays the very next business day. So you need to own $DIVO before the open on the ex-date (for November, that's Friday, November 27, the day after Thanksgiving) to get that month's check. The cash lands fast: one business day later.

That's the opposite end of the month from $GPIX and $JEPI, which both go ex in the first days of the month. If you own $DIVO next to one of them, you get paid at two different points in the month. And if you hold $SCHD too, its remaining 2026 ex-date is December 9 (see the $SCHD 2026 dividend dates).

Ignore the December spike for a second and look at the regular check. The average monthly payout went from about $0.158 in 2024 to $0.170 in the first ten months of 2025 to $0.186 in 2026, growth of roughly 7% to 9% a year. October 2026's $0.19132 is about 5% above October 2025's $0.18152. That's the kind of payout growth I want from an income fund, and it's rare in the covered call world, where a lot of funds slowly cut their checks.

The December 2025 Check: $0.95 Instead of $0.19

On December 30, 2025, $DIVO went ex for $0.95339 per share, about five times a normal month. November 2025 was also a bit higher than usual at $0.21394.

Year-end jumps like that in an actively managed fund usually come from realized gains being paid out, not from a permanent raise. In December 2024 there was no jump at all ($0.16464), so don't budget for a repeat. Amplify's 19a-1 notices and your 1099-DIV show how each payment is classified.

This is the part most people miss when they look up "$DIVO yield." Include that December check and the trailing 12-month total is about $3.03 per share, which looks like a 6.4% yield at $47.64. Strip it out and the regular run-rate is closer to 4.8%, right in line with Amplify's 4.88% distribution rate. I'd plan around the lower number.

Here's what I actually look at before buying any income ETF: does it pay reliably, has the price decayed, and what's the total return. $DIVO has returned about 12.4% a year since 2016. That's behind the S&P 500, and I won't pretend otherwise. Selling calls and owning only 20-something stocks means you'll trail the index in strong bull markets.

But compare it with what most monthly income funds deliver. A 12% annual return with a growing monthly check and no price decay is a really solid result. I'd rather own a fund like this than chase a 12% yield that quietly eats the principal.

The Risks I'd Want You to Know

  • It's still a stock fund. When the market drops, $DIVO drops. Covered call income cushions the fall a bit, it doesn't stop it.
  • Concentrated. About 20 to 25 core stocks, with financials around 26% and tech around 18% of the fund as of September 30, 2026. A bad stretch for big banks would hurt.
  • Capped upside. When CWP sells calls, it gives up gains above the strike on those stocks. That's a big reason it has lagged the S&P 500.
  • Active management. The results depend on the managers' stock picks and call timing. Kevin Simpson and Josh Smith have run it since inception, which I like, but that's a people risk too.
  • Lumpy year-end payments. The December 2025 check shows distributions can vary a lot from year to year. Budget off the regular monthly check.

My Takeaway

$DIVO pays monthly, goes ex near the end of the month, and pays one business day later. Mark Friday, November 27 as the next ex-date and Wednesday, December 30 for the last one of 2026. The regular check has grown roughly 7% to 9% a year, the share price hasn't decayed, and the fund has compounded at about 12% a year since 2016.

It won't give you the biggest yield in the room. That's the point. $DIVO is for the income investor who wants a monthly check that grows, without giving up on growing the principal. I'd treat it as a core holding candidate, not a yield play.
