What $CONY Actually Is

$CONY is the YieldMax COIN Option Income Strategy ETF. It launched on August 14, 2023 and it's built around one stock: Coinbase Global ($COIN), the crypto exchange.

Here's the part most people miss. $CONY isn't a crypto fund and it doesn't simply hold $COIN shares. YieldMax describes it as an actively managed ETF that sells call spreads on $COIN to harvest option premium from the stock's volatility, while keeping some participation in $COIN's upside. That premium is where the weekly check comes from. And $COIN is one of the most volatile large stocks in the market, which is why the yield is so high.

The fund manages about $218 million (November 23, 2026), charges a 1.04% gross expense ratio, switched from monthly to weekly payouts in October 2025, and did a 1-for-10 reverse split effective December 2, 2025. If you've looked at YieldMax's other big single-stock funds, I did the same breakdown for the $MSTY dividend history and the $AMDY dividend history.

When is the next $CONY dividend?

This is Thanksgiving week, and Thursday, November 26 is a market holiday, so the usual Thursday ex-date can't happen. Last year YieldMax declared the Thanksgiving payout on Wednesday, set the ex-date for Friday, and paid the following Monday. If it follows that pattern again, expect an ex-date around Friday, November 27, 2026 and payment around Monday, November 30. That's my read of the pattern, not an announcement, and the amount isn't known until it's declared.

To get a week's check you need to own $CONY before the market opens on the ex-date. Buying on the ex-date itself is too late for that week.

Why do older $CONY payouts look so small (or so big)?

Two changes make the long history confusing. First, $CONY paid monthly until October 2025; the last monthly check went ex on October 16, 2025 and the first weekly one on October 23, 2025. Second, YieldMax did a 1-for-10 reverse split effective December 2, 2025 (announced November 26, 2025). YieldMax's table shows the pre-split checks at their original size, so the five weekly payouts from October 23 to November 28, 2025 show up as $0.06 to $0.13. Multiply those by 10 to compare them with today's shares. Same idea for the monthly checks from 2024, which ran from about $1 to $2.79 per old share, or roughly $10 to $28 per share at today's count. Don't line up old and new checks in raw dollars.

The CONY Dividend Yield: 63% or 159%?

YieldMax lists a 63.01% distribution rate as of November 18, 2026. That number takes the newest weekly check, multiplies it by 52 and divides by NAV. Our live list shows about 63% too. The 30-day SEC yield, which only counts net investment income and leaves out option income, was 3.55% as of October 31.

Look backward and you get a wild number. The last 52 weekly payouts add up to about $18.16 per share (counting the pre-split November 28, 2025 check at its split-adjusted size), roughly 159% of today's $11.41 price. A fund doesn't pay out 159% of its price in a year and stay healthy. That trailing figure is high because the share price collapsed underneath the checks.

Here's what I actually look at instead: return of capital. YieldMax estimates that 19 of the 47 payouts in 2026 were 90% or more return of capital, while 10 were estimated at 0%. The latest check was estimated at about 94% ROC. ROC isn't automatically bad, since it lowers your cost basis instead of being taxed right away. But on a fund whose price keeps falling, it's a strong hint that a lot of the "yield" is your own money coming back. The 1099 at year end settles the real tax character. The Risks I'd Want You to Know

  • One stock, and a volatile one. $CONY lives and dies with $COIN. YieldMax's own disclosure: the fund captures only a portion of $COIN's gains and is subject to all of its potential losses, which the option premium may not offset.
  • Capped upside. Selling call spreads means big $COIN rallies get partly sold away, while sharp drops hit in full.
  • Shrinking checks. The weekly payout fell from $0.6138 in March to $0.1484 in November, and it's been cut 10 weeks running.
  • Return of capital. About 40% of the 2026 payouts were estimated to be mostly ROC. Watch your cost basis.
  • Reverse splits. The 1-for-10 split in December 2025 didn't change the value of anyone's position, but it's what happens when a share price falls so far. Price decay is the long-term story here.

My Takeaway

$CONY pays weekly: declared Wednesday, ex-date Thursday, paid Friday, with Thanksgiving week likely shifting the next one. It has paid about $14.98 per share across 47 checks in 2026, and the latest was $0.1484, the smallest of the year after 10 straight cuts. The 63% distribution rate is real cash.

But the total return is the scoreboard, and it's ugly: about -52% over one year with every check reinvested. I'd never treat $CONY as the foundation of an income portfolio. If you want a Coinbase bet that spins off cash, size it small and take the checks knowing a lot of it is your own money coming back. I'd rather build my core on funds with no price decay, and keep something like this as a small satellite at most.
