How This List Works

📐 Annualized return, explained in one line

We take each fund's total return since inception (price change plus every distribution) and convert it to a compound annual growth rate: the steady yearly return that would have produced the same result. That lets a 16-year-old fund and a 2-year-old fund sit in the same table. It's the same calculation shown in the Annualized column of the PRO terminal, recalculated every time this page loads.

  • To make the main list, a fund needs at least one full year of trading history and an annualized return above $VOO's.
  • Funds under one year old go in the On Deck table. Annualizing a few months of returns produces eye-popping numbers that don't mean much yet.
  • Track record matters. We tag every fund with its age and give the 3+ year funds their own table, because beating $VOO for 4 years is a different achievement than beating it for 14 months.

⚡ A Word on the Single-Stock Rockets

Many of the biggest annualized numbers on this page come from option income funds tied to one stock. They've ridden huge runs in their underlying shares, and many also show price decay, meaning their share price has fallen since launch and the total return comes from distributions. That can reverse quickly if the underlying stock turns. Use the "No price decay" filter above to separate them out.

The Catch: Why Young Funds Look So Good Against $VOO

This list is a fun, honest scoreboard, but it compares different time periods. $VOO's … covers … years that include the 2020 crash and the 2022 bear market. A fund launched in the last year or two may have only lived through a strong stretch for stocks. If $VOO were measured over the same short window, its annualized number would often be higher than its long-run annualized rate.

  • Short records swing wildly. One great year annualizes into a huge number; one bad year can knock a fund right off this list.
  • Concentration cuts both ways. Sector and single-stock funds can beat $VOO by a mile, or trail it by a mile, depending on one industry or one company.
  • Price decay matters for income investors. A fund can beat $VOO on total return while its share price shrinks. Filter by "No price decay" above to see which funds did both.
  • Taxes aren't included. High distributions can be taxed very differently from $VOO's small, mostly qualified dividends.

⚠️ Past Performance Isn't a Promise

Every number on this page is backward-looking. Beating $VOO so far does not mean a fund will keep beating it. Use this list as a starting point for research, not as a buy list.
