Which Roundhill ETF Should You Pick?

If you want weekly income from a broad index, $QDTE, $XDTE and $RDTE are the 0DTE trio, but all three show price decay on our list.

The WeeklyPay single-stock funds are really a bet on one company with 1.2x leverage. $AMDW and $GOOW have worked; $COIW and $TSLW have not.

If you want weekly pay without stock risk, $WEEK holds T-bills.

This is educational, not a recommendation. The right fund depends on your goals, your taxes and how much volatility you can stomach.

The Risks I'd Want You to Know

  • Price decay. Our list flags price decay on $COIW, $YETH, $YBTC, $QDTE, $TOPW, $PLTW, $MAGY, $TSLW, $RDTE, $XDTE. A big yield doesn't help if the share price keeps sliding.
  • Bond and T-bill funds behave differently. $WEEK is a bond / T-bill fund, driven by interest rates and credit, not the stock market.
  • Capped upside on option funds. Selling calls trades away part of the upside for income, so covered call funds lag in big rallies.
  • Variable payouts. Option income funds pay different amounts each period. Budget off the yearly total, not one big check.
  • Small funds. $COIW has under $50 million in assets, which can mean wider trading spreads and closure risk.
  • Taxes. Option income is often taxed as ordinary income, and some payouts are return of capital. Check your 1099 and talk to a tax pro.

My Takeaway

If you're searching for Roundhill's highest yielding ETF, the answer is $COIW at roughly 63%, paid weekly, but with price decay on our list. The best total return since launch in the group belongs to $AMDW at +297%.

Here's how I think about it. I don't buy an income ETF because of the yield number alone. I want three things: reliable payouts, no price decay, and a total return I can live with. A 10% yield that keeps your principal intact beats a 40% yield that slowly melts.
