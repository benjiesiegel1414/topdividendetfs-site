How We Grade ETFs

Every ETF on TopDividendETFs.com and TopDividendETFsPRO gets a letter grade from A+ to F. Here's exactly how that grade is built, so you know what's behind it.

Each fund gets a score out of 100 based on six things I look at as a long-term income investor. The score turns into a letter grade. The same rules apply to every ETF, and the grades are reviewed every two weeks as new prices and payouts come in.

The six factors

1. Total return vs. the market 25 points

How much the fund has grown per year since it launched, with every distribution reinvested, compared with the S&P 500 over the same period. Funds that keep up with or beat the market score highest. T-bill funds are compared with T-bills and bond funds with the U.S. bond market, since comparing them to stocks wouldn't be fair.

2. Income paid back 25 points

What a buy-and-hold investor actually got: every distribution paid since launch plus the change in the share price, per year, without reinvesting. A fund can have some price decay and still score well here if it has paid out a lot of income over the years.

3. Price decay 15 points

Where the share price is today compared with where it started. A fund whose price has grown since launch scores highest. The more the price has eroded, the lower the score.

4. Payout consistency 15 points

How steady the payouts have been over the last two years. Big swings from one payout to the next lower the score, and so does income that fell more than 20% from the prior year. Funds that grow their dividend 5% a year or more get a bonus.

5. Tax efficiency 10 points

Based on the fund's Tax Treatment Grade, which reflects how tax-friendly it is for someone holding it in a taxable account. More tax-efficient funds score higher.

6. Income or dividend growth 10 points

Either the current yield or how fast the dividend has grown over the last three years, whichever is stronger. A high-income fund and a fast-growing dividend payer can both earn these points.

Track record

New funds need time to prove themselves, so a short history caps the grade:

  • Under 1 year: the highest possible grade is B
  • Under 3 years: the highest possible grade is A-
  • Under 5 years: the highest possible grade is A
  • A+ requires at least 5 years of history

What an A+ looks like

An A+ fund has no price decay, has kept up with or beaten the market over time, pays steady or growing income, and is tax-efficient. Funds like $OVL and $SCHG are good examples: both have grown a lot over time, and both have paid investors well along the way, $OVL through high income and $SCHG through dividend growth.

Where the data comes from

Total returns, share prices and distribution history come from daily market data. Yields come from each fund issuer's own website, and the Tax Treatment Grade and price decay come from the TopDividendETFs database. Grades are recalculated every two weeks.
