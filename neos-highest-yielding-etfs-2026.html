Which NEOS ETF Should You Pick?

If you want the NEOS funds with the strongest track record, $QQQI and $SPYI are the core holdings: big, liquid and no price decay on our list.

If you want more upside with a hedge, $QQQH and $SPYH trade yield for protection. For a cash-like position, $CSHI is a T-bill fund.

The Bitcoin and Boosted funds pay the most, but they're leveraged or crypto-driven and already flagged for price decay. Size them small if you use them at all.

This is educational, not a recommendation. The right fund depends on your goals, your taxes and how much volatility you can stomach.

The Risks I'd Want You to Know

  • Price decay. Our list flags price decay on $XBCI, $BTCI, $XQQI, $XSPI, $IWMI, $IAUI, $IYRI, $BNDI. A big yield doesn't help if the share price keeps sliding.
  • Bond and T-bill funds behave differently. $BNDI, $CSHI are bond or T-bill funds, driven by interest rates and credit, not the stock market.
  • Capped upside on option funds. Selling calls trades away part of the upside for income, so covered call funds lag in big rallies.
  • Variable payouts. Option income funds pay different amounts each period. Budget off the yearly total, not one big check.
  • Short history. $XBCI, $XQQI, $XSPI, $MLPI have less than a year of data.
  • Small funds. $SPYH has under $50 million in assets, which can mean wider trading spreads and closure risk.
  • Taxes. Option income is often taxed as ordinary income, and some payouts are return of capital. Check your 1099 and talk to a tax pro.

My Takeaway

If you're searching for NEOS's highest yielding ETF, the answer is $XBCI at roughly 41%, paid monthly, but with price decay on our list. The best total return since launch in the group belongs to $SPYI at +79%.

Here's how I think about it. I don't buy an income ETF because of the yield number alone. I want three things: reliable payouts, no price decay, and a total return I can live with. A 10% yield that keeps your principal intact beats a 40% yield that slowly melts.
