The short version

If you want the biggest State Street check, it's $XLEI, but that's a single-sector energy bet. $XLKI does the same with tech. $SPHY is a high-yield bond fund with price decay on our list, and $SDY is a 20-year dividend growth fund with a small yield and a long track record.

What it holds and how it pays: It owns shares of the Energy Select Sector SPDR ETF ($XLE) and sells call options on it. State Street runs it as an active strategy built for current income while keeping some long-term growth potential.

The trade-off: You're making a bet on one sector. Energy stocks swing with oil prices, and selling calls caps the upside when energy rips. It's also barely over a year old, so the track record is short.

3. $SPHY: SPDR Portfolio High Yield Bond HIGH-YIELD BOND FUND PRICE DECAY: YES

$SPHY (State Street SPDR Portfolio High Yield Bond ETF)

Yield: ~7.43% · Assets: $11.8B · Inception: June 18, 2012 · Expense: 0.05% · Total Return Since Launch: +95% · Pays: Monthly

What it holds and how it pays: It tracks the ICE BofA US High Yield Index, so it owns a big basket of junk bonds. The income is just the interest those bonds pay.

The trade-off: This is a bond fund, not a stock fund, and our list flags price decay on it. High-yield bonds pay well but default risk and rising rates can chip away at the share price. The 0.05% fee is about as cheap as it gets.

4. $SDY: SPDR S&P Dividend

$SDY (SPDR S&P Dividend ETF)

Yield: ~2.4% · Assets: $22.0B · Inception: November 8, 2005 · Expense: 0.35% · Total Return Since Launch: +511% · Pays: Quarterly

What it holds and how it pays: It tracks the S&P High Yield Dividend Aristocrats Index: companies that have raised their dividend for at least 20 straight years. Income comes from those dividends, paid quarterly.

The trade-off: The yield is modest, but this is a real dividend growth fund with a 20-year history. It's the opposite of an option income fund: lower check today, more growth over time.

Which State Street ETF Should You Pick?

If you want maximum income and you're fine with sector risk, $XLEI and $XLKI are the high yielders. Just know they're young and concentrated.

If you want income from bonds instead of stocks, $SPHY is cheap at 0.05%, but watch that price decay flag.

If you care more about growing income over decades than about today's yield, $SDY is the one with the long history.

This is educational, not a recommendation. The right fund depends on your goals, your taxes and how much volatility you can stomach.

The Risks I'd Want You to Know

  • Price decay. Our list flags price decay on $SPHY. A big yield doesn't help if the share price keeps sliding.
  • Bond and T-bill funds behave differently. $SPHY is a bond / T-bill fund, driven by interest rates and credit, not the stock market.
  • Capped upside on option funds. Selling calls trades away part of the upside for income, so covered call funds lag in big rallies.
  • Variable payouts. Option income funds pay different amounts each period. Budget off the yearly total, not one big check.
  • Small funds. $XLKI has under $50 million in assets, which can mean wider trading spreads and closure risk.
  • Taxes. Option income is often taxed as ordinary income, and some payouts are return of capital. Check your 1099 and talk to a tax pro.

My Takeaway

If you're searching for State Street's highest yielding ETF, the answer is $XLEI at roughly 20.8%, paid monthly, with no price decay on our list. The best total return since launch in the group belongs to $SDY at +511%.

Here's how I think about it. I don't buy an income ETF because of the yield number alone. I want three things: reliable payouts, no price decay, and a total return I can live with. A 10% yield that keeps your principal intact beats a 40% yield that slowly melts.
