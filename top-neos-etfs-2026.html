Why I keep this NEOS list updated

I get asked about NEOS funds more than almost any other issuer. $QQQI and $SPYI are two of the most popular income ETFs on the planet right now, and NEOS keeps launching new versions of the same idea on gold, bitcoin, real estate, energy, international stocks and bonds.

The problem is that most lists only show you the yield. That is the number that gets clicks, and it is also the number that gets people hurt. A 41% distribution rate means nothing if the share price is falling faster than the checks are coming in.

So this page shows both. Every NEOS fund we track, ranked by current distribution rate from highest to lowest, with its total return since inception sitting right next to it. Both numbers come straight from the same database that powers TopDividendETFs PRO, which we refresh several times a week from the issuer's own fund pages and market data. When the database updates, this page updates. You don't have to wait for me to rewrite it.

How to read the table: the distribution rate is what the fund is paying right now, annualized from its most recent monthly distribution. The total return is what someone who bought at launch has actually earned, share price change plus every distribution reinvested. The first number is a promise about income. The second number is the receipt.

Every NEOS ETF, one by one

In order of current distribution rate. Numbers in bold update from our database.

1. $XBCI: NEOS Boosted Bitcoin High Income ETF

Distribution rate 41.0% · Total return -8% · Launched Feb 2026

The highest distribution rate in the entire NEOS family. It takes the bitcoin income idea from $BTCI and pushes the option strategy harder for a bigger monthly payment. It's also one of the youngest funds on the list, and so far the total return hasn't kept up with the payout. This is a bitcoin bet with an income wrapper, not a bond replacement.

2. $BTCI: NEOS Bitcoin High Income ETF

Distribution rate 25.0% · Total return +7% · Launched Oct 2024

The original NEOS bitcoin fund and the biggest of the crypto income funds by assets. It holds bitcoin exposure and sells options against it for monthly income. When bitcoin runs, you'll lag it. When bitcoin drops, the distributions won't save you. Compare its total return to its rate before you buy it for the yield.

3. $XQQI: NEOS Boosted Nasdaq-100 High Income ETF

Distribution rate 20.7% · Total return +1% · Launched Jan 2026

The boosted version of $QQQI. Same Nasdaq-100 index, a more aggressive option structure, a higher check and a higher fee (0.98% vs 0.68%). The trade-off is less upside when tech rallies. Put its total return next to $QQQI's and you'll see what you're giving up for the extra income.

4. $XSPI: NEOS Boosted S&P 500 High Income ETF

Distribution rate 17.0% · Total return +8% · Launched Jan 2026

The boosted version of $SPYI. Same deal as $XQQI: more income now, a higher expense ratio, and less participation in big up moves. Fine if you need the cash flow today, but I'd want to see a lot more history before treating it like $SPYI.

5. $IWMI: NEOS Russell 2000 High Income ETF

Distribution rate 14.2% · Total return +36% · Launched Jun 2024

Small caps with the NEOS option overlay. It pays a little more than $QQQI and has put up a solid total return since launch. Small caps are more volatile than the big indexes, so expect bigger swings in the share price.

6. $QQQI: NEOS Nasdaq-100 High Income ETF

Distribution rate 14.1% · Total return +48% · Launched Jan 2024

The biggest fund NEOS runs and one of the most popular income ETFs anywhere. Nasdaq-100 stocks plus index call options, paid monthly. This is the fund most people mean when they say "NEOS." I track every payment on the $QQQI dividend history page and the upcoming schedule on the $QQQI 2026 dividend dates page. Torn between it and the S&P 500 version? I put them head to head in $SPYI vs $QQQI.

7. $MLPI: NEOS MLP & Energy Infrastructure High Income ETF

Distribution rate 13.8% · Total return +14% · Launched Dec 2025

Pipelines and energy infrastructure with an option overlay on top. Energy MLPs already pay well on their own, so this one stacks two income sources. It's still young, so judge it on a full year of data, not a few months.

8. $IAUI: NEOS Gold High Income ETF

Distribution rate 12.1% · Total return +11% · Launched Jun 2025

Gold doesn't pay a dividend, so NEOS manufactures one with options. Interesting for diversification, but remember the income comes from selling away some of gold's upside. If gold takes off, plain gold will beat it.

9. $SPYI: NEOS S&P 500 High Income ETF

Distribution rate 11.4% · Total return +73% · Launched Aug 2022

The fund that started it all for NEOS, and the one with the longest track record of the core lineup. It's ninth on distribution rate and first on total return. That's not a coincidence: it has had the most time to compound, and its overlay leaves room for the S&P 500 to grow underneath. See $SPYI's 2026 dividend dates for the payment calendar.

10. $IYRI: NEOS Real Estate High Income ETF

Distribution rate 10.8% · Total return +14% · Launched Jan 2025

REITs plus the option overlay. One note for taxable accounts: REIT income is generally taxed less favorably than index option income, which is part of why this fund's tax profile looks different from the core equity funds. PRO members can see the Tax Treatment column for this one.

11. $NIHI: NEOS MSCI EAFE High Income ETF

Distribution rate 10.2% · Total return +12% · Launched Sep 2025

International developed markets (Europe, Japan, Australia and more) with monthly income. A good way to add non-US exposure to an income portfolio that's otherwise all S&P 500 and Nasdaq.

12. $QQQH: NEOS Nasdaq-100 Hedged Equity Income ETF

Distribution rate 9.0% · Total return +18%

Nasdaq-100 exposure with downside protection built in. You give up some income compared to $QQQI in exchange for a cushion when tech sells off. Built for people who want income but hate drawdowns.

13. $SPYH: NEOS S&P 500 Hedged Equity Income ETF

Distribution rate 7.8% · Total return +28% · Launched Apr 2025

The hedged cousin of $SPYI. Lower rate, more protection. Still small by assets, so keep an eye on trading spreads if you buy it.

14. $BNDI: NEOS Enhanced Income Aggregate Bond ETF

Distribution rate 5.8% · Total return +15% · Launched Aug 2022

A core bond portfolio with an options strategy layered on top for extra income. It pays noticeably more than a plain aggregate bond fund, which is the whole pitch.

15. $CSHI: NEOS Enhanced Income Cash Alternative ETF

Distribution rate 5.0% · Total return +24% · Launched Aug 2022

Short-term Treasury bills plus an option overlay. Think of it as a cash parking spot that tries to pay a bit more than a money market fund. NEOS now markets it as the "Enhanced Income Cash Alternative" fund. It has been one of the most consistent funds in the lineup.

How NEOS distributions are taxed (the short version)

This is a big reason NEOS funds are so popular in taxable accounts. Most of the equity funds sell options on broad indexes, which generally fall under Section 1256: gains are treated as 60% long term and 40% short term no matter how long the fund held the position. NEOS also harvests losses inside the funds, which has pushed a large share of many distributions into return of capital. ROC isn't taxed the year you receive it. It lowers your cost basis instead, so the tax shows up when you sell.

That's real value in a regular brokerage account and worth nothing extra in a Roth IRA. Tax character changes every year and differs by fund. I broke down the actual 19a-1 and Form 8937 numbers in why NEOS ETFs are "tax friendly". Talk to a tax professional about your own situation.

What can go wrong

No fund family is a free lunch. Here's the honest list.

  • 📉
    Capped upside in strong markets. The option overlay is where the income comes from, and it's also why these funds lag their plain index in a fast rally. That's the design, not a malfunction.
  • 🪙
    Crypto funds carry crypto risk. $BTCI and $XBCI move with bitcoin. A 25% or 40% distribution rate does not protect you from a big drop in bitcoin.
  • 💸
    Distributions change every month. Option premiums depend on volatility. When markets calm down, payments usually shrink. Never budget off one month's number.
  • 🏷️
    Boosted funds cost more. 0.98% vs 0.68% is a meaningful difference over years, on top of giving up more upside.
  • 🆕
    Many funds are young. A total return over a few months tells you very little. Give the newer funds a full market cycle before trusting the numbers.
  • 🧾
    Return of capital is deferral, not tax-free. It lowers your cost basis, so the bill arrives when you sell.

NEOS ETF FAQ

Which NEOS ETF has the highest distribution rate?

$XBCI, the NEOS Boosted Bitcoin High Income ETF, at 41.0%. $BTCI is second and $XQQI is third. Among non-crypto funds, $XQQI and $XSPI lead, followed by $IWMI and $QQQI.

Which NEOS ETF has the best total return?

$SPYI, up 73% since its August 2022 launch with distributions reinvested. $QQQI and $IWMI follow. The highest-yielding funds have some of the weakest total returns.

How many NEOS ETFs are there?

NEOS lists 19 ETFs. We track 15 of them in our live database, and the other four ($NEHI, $HYBI, $TLTI and $NLSI) are listed separately above.

Do NEOS ETFs pay monthly?

Yes. Every NEOS fund on this page pays monthly, which is why they show up all over MonthlyETFs.com.

What's the difference between $QQQI and $XQQI?

Both are built on the Nasdaq-100. $QQQI is the standard version at a 0.68% expense ratio. $XQQI is the boosted version at 0.98%, with a more aggressive option strategy that pays more each month and gives up more upside.

Is a high distribution rate the same as a high return?

No. The distribution rate is what a fund pays out. Total return is what you actually earned after the share price change. A fund can pay 40% a year and still lose money if its share price falls faster than it pays. That's why this page shows both.
