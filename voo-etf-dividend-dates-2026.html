When Does VOO Pay Its Dividend in 2026?

The Vanguard S&P 500 ETF ($VOO) pays dividends quarterly, in March, June, September and December. Below is the complete VOO dividend schedule for 2026, with every ex-dividend date, record date, payable date and the per-share amount, based on Vanguard's declared distributions.

To receive a VOO dividend, you must own shares before the ex-dividend date. Buy on or after the ex-date and that quarter's dividend goes to the seller. VOO usually pays two to three business days after the ex-date, one of the quickest turnarounds of any major ETF.

So far in 2026, VOO has paid $1.8724 in Q1 and $1.9622 in Q2, and declared $1.8226 for Q3, a year-to-date total of $5.66 per share. Over the trailing twelve months VOO has paid about $7.43 per share, a yield of roughly 1.05% at a share price near $711.

Why VOO's Dividend Keeps Growing

VOO owns every company in the S&P 500, so its dividend is simply the sum of what roughly 500 of America's largest companies pay out. As those companies grow their earnings and raise their own dividends, VOO's payout rises with them. The only down year in the last decade was 2020, when many companies paused or trimmed dividends during the pandemic, and VOO's payout was back above its 2019 level by 2022.

The Vanguard S&P 500 ETF ($VOO) is one of the largest ETFs in the world, with roughly $1.09 trillion in assets. It launched on September 7, 2010 and tracks the S&P 500, holding about 500 of the largest US companies. Its biggest positions are Nvidia (about 8.1%), Apple (about 7.0%) and Microsoft (about 5.7%).

VOO charges just 0.03% a year, or $3 on every $10,000 invested. Its dividend yield is low because most S&P 500 companies reinvest the bulk of their profits instead of paying them out. Investors own VOO mainly for total return: it has averaged about 14.9% a year since launch with dividends reinvested.

How to Use the VOO Dividend Schedule

Three dates decide whether you get a VOO dividend and when it lands in your account.

Understanding the Three Key Dates

  • Ex-Dividend Date: You must own VOO before this date to get the dividend. Buying on or after the ex-date means you miss that quarter.
  • Record Date: The day Vanguard records who is eligible. Since US markets moved to one-day settlement, VOO's record date falls on the same day as its ex-date.
  • Payable Date: The day the cash hits your brokerage account, usually two to three business days after the ex-date for VOO.
  1. Find the next VOO ex-dividend dateThe next one is September 28, 2026. Own shares before that date to qualify for the $1.8226 Q3 payment.
  2. Buy by the close the trading day beforeFor Q3 2026 that was Friday, September 25. Miss it and your next chance is the Q4 dividend in December.
  3. Hold through the ex-dateAfter the ex-date you can sell and still receive the dividend.
  4. Collect your paymentVOO's Q3 dividend is credited on September 30, 2026. The Q4 payment is expected in late December.

💡 Dividend Reinvestment (DRIP) and Taxes

Most brokerages can automatically reinvest your VOO dividends into more shares. With a yield near 1%, reinvesting won't change your income much in any single year, but it adds meaningfully to long-term total return. Most of VOO's dividends have historically been qualified dividends, taxed at the lower long-term capital gains rates if you meet the holding period.
