The problem: your dividends are scattered

Most income investors I talk to don't have one account. They have a Roth IRA at Fidelity, a taxable account at Schwab, an old 401(k), maybe an HSA and a Robinhood account they opened years ago. Each broker shows you its own dividends, and none of them show you the number you actually care about: how much income your whole portfolio pays you every month.

So you end up with a spreadsheet. And the spreadsheet is always a little wrong. You forget a DRIP, you miss a special dividend, or your monthly payers like $JEPI, $JEPQ and $QQQI change their distribution and your projection is off again.

The real issue is accuracy. A lot of dividend trackers estimate your income from a generic data feed instead of what your broker actually paid. That's how you end up with "income" numbers that don't match your account.

The fix: one tracker connected to every account

What you want is a tracker that plugs straight into your brokerages and records the dividends that actually land. That's the reason I like MerryDiv. According to MerryDiv, dividend payments and history come directly from your brokerage via Plaid, so they match what hit your account. Fund yields use trailing 12-month cash distributions, which is how I'd want monthly income ETFs measured anyway.

Step 1: Create a free account

Sign up at merrydiv.com. The free plan already gives you unlimited manual accounts and holdings, so you can look around before paying anything. If you want automatic sync, start the 14-day free trial on a paid plan.

Step 2: Link your brokerages through Plaid

Click Link account and pick your broker. Plaid handles the login, and the connection is read-only: MerryDiv can see your holdings and dividend payments, but it can't trade or move money, and your brokerage credentials aren't stored on MerryDiv's servers. Repeat for each account. Plaid covers 10,000+ US and Canadian institutions, including Schwab, Fidelity, Vanguard, Robinhood, E*TRADE and Merrill Edge.

Step 3: Watch your dividend history fill in

Your holdings and past payouts sync in on their own. Your Roth IRA, 401(k), taxable account and HSA all roll into one dividend feed, and the dashboard shows your average monthly income, your best month and whether your income is growing.

Step 4: Check the dividend calendar

The 12-month dividend calendar shows what's coming and when, and the dashboard keeps a running total for the next 30 days. This is the view I check most, because it tells me what cash I'll have to reinvest.

Step 5: Run Portfolio X-Ray

If you own ETFs, you probably own the same companies two or three times over. X-Ray looks inside your ETFs and mutual funds and adds up your real exposure to each company across every account.
