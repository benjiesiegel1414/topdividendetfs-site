QUALCOMM Just Became SCHD's Largest Holding

If you hold the Schwab U.S. Dividend Equity ETF ($SCHD), the top of your fund looks very different this week. In the September quarterly rebalance, effective around September 21, 2026, the index that $SCHD tracks reset every holding's weight back toward its target. The result was one of the more dramatic reshuffles in recent memory, and it happened without a single stock being added or removed.

$QCOM (QUALCOMM) is the headline. It climbed from the 16th-largest position all the way to #1, with its weight jumping from 2.68% to 4.74%. Schwab added roughly 60% more $QCOM shares to hit that target. $TXN (Texas Instruments) made a similar leap, from 13th to #2, rising from 3.22% to 4.41% on a share count up about 32%. The fund's old top three, all healthcare names, were pushed down the list.

🔁 Why Did This Happen? How SCHD's Rebalance Works

$SCHD tracks the Dow Jones U.S. Dividend 100 Index. That index picks its 100 stocks once a year at the annual reconstitution in the spring, and between those events no new names are added. That is why a September rebalance never brings in fresh tickers or removes anyone. It only moves the existing weights around.

Every quarter the index resets each holding back toward a target weight built on a modified market-cap-and-yield formula, with a roughly 4% cap per stock and a 25% cap per sector. Between resets, weights drift with price. When a stock runs up it floats overweight and gets trimmed. When a stock lags it drifts underweight and gets topped up. That is the whole story: $MRK, $ABT and $AMGN had appreciated and drifted overweight, so they were sold down, while $QCOM and $TXN had become relatively cheap for their yield and were bought.
