The 30-Second Version

  • No stocks were added or removed. Every holding carried over from before, so this was a weight-only rebalance
  • $QCOM nearly doubled its weight from 2.68% to 4.74% and is now the fund's single largest position, up from 16th place. Schwab added roughly 60% more $QCOM shares
  • $TXN jumped from 13th to 2nd, climbing from 3.22% to 4.41%, with the share count up about 32%
  • The former top three got trimmed hard. $MRK, $ABT and $AMGN were each cut by 0.7 to 0.8 percentage points
  • Net effect: more semiconductors, less healthcare. The fund tilted toward chips and away from its big pharma names

Why Did This Happen?

A quick primer on how SCHD's index actually works.

$SCHD tracks the Dow Jones U.S. Dividend 100 Index. That index chooses its 100 stocks once a year at the annual reconstitution in the spring, and between those annual events no new names are added. That is why a September rebalance never brings in fresh tickers or kicks anyone out. It only moves the existing weights around, which is exactly what we saw here.

Every quarter the index resets each holding back toward a target weight based on a modified market-cap-and-yield formula, with a roughly 4% cap on any single stock and a 25% cap on any single sector. Between those resets, weights drift with price. When a stock runs up, it floats overweight and gets trimmed at the next rebalance. When a stock lags, it drifts underweight and gets topped up.

That is the whole story behind this quarter. The healthcare leaders $MRK, $ABT and $AMGN had appreciated and drifted overweight, so the rebalance sold them down. $QCOM and $TXN had become relatively cheap for their yield and fundamentals, so the index bought a lot more of them. The caps are applied using reference-date prices, which is why $QCOM can show above 4% in the live holdings while still being a capped name.
