When Does JEPI Pay Its Dividend in 2026?

The JPMorgan Equity Premium Income ETF (JEPI) distributes monthly. The ex-dividend date is normally the first business day of the month, with the record date falling on the same day and the pay date roughly two business days later.

JEPI's September 2026 distribution came in at $0.37142 per share, with an ex-date of September 1 and a pay date of September 3. That is a 1.3% step up from August's $0.36664, but it sits well below the $0.44761 paid in May, which remains the high point of 2026 so far.

Across 2026 so far, JEPI has paid $3.08 per share over eight distributions, an average of $0.3848 per month. That is fractionally below 2025's $0.3933 monthly average — 2026 is tracking as a roughly flat year for JEPI income, not a record one. Over the trailing twelve months JEPI has distributed $4.58 per share, a yield of roughly 8.1% at a share price near $56.

One quirk catches investors out every year: JEPI has never had a January ex-dividend date. Not once since 2020. The explanation is below.

⚠️ Why JEPI Has No January Ex-Dividend Date

Look at any JEPI calendar and January appears to be missing. It isn't — JEPI runs two December ex-dividend dates every year. One falls at the start of December as normal, and a second falls in the final days of the month. That late-December distribution has a pay date in early January, which is the payment shareholders receive as their January income.

This has happened every single year since the fund launched:

  • Ex Dec 30, 2020 → paid Jan 5, 2021 ($0.5428)
  • Ex Dec 30, 2021 → paid Jan 4, 2022 ($0.4586)
  • Ex Dec 29, 2022 → paid Jan 4, 2023 ($0.57292)
  • Ex Dec 28, 2023 → paid Jan 3, 2024 ($0.42781)
  • Ex Dec 31, 2024 → paid Jan 3, 2025 ($0.3948)
  • Ex Dec 31, 2025 → paid Jan 5, 2026 ($0.42709)

In 75 distributions, JEPI has never once had a January ex-dividend date. The practical consequence: if you buy JEPI in early January expecting to catch a January ex-date, you have already missed it. The relevant ex-date was in late December. JPMorgan has already published December 31, 2026 as the next one.

📌 The December 15, 2026 Capital Gains Date

JPMorgan's published 2026 calendar includes an extra reserved date — an ex-date of December 15, 2026 with a pay date of December 17 — flagged as a potential capital gains distribution. It currently carries a $0.0000 placeholder, which means the date is held open but nothing has been declared.

This is routine housekeeping rather than a signal. Funds reserve a capital gains date each year in case realized gains need to be passed through to shareholders for tax purposes. JEPI's distribution record shows no capital gains distributions to date — every payment in its history has been an income distribution. The date may well pass with nothing paid, as it has in prior years.

Worth watching if you hold JEPI in a taxable account, since a capital gains distribution would arrive as a separate taxable event in mid-December on top of the regular monthly payment.

What the Year-by-Year Numbers Actually Show

2022 was JEPI's outlier year, not its baseline. The $6.36 paid that year came out of a genuine bear market with elevated implied volatility for months on end — exactly the conditions that make selling call options lucrative. Income investors who bought JEPI in late 2022 expecting $6+ per share annually have been disappointed every year since.

Strip 2022 out and JEPI's full years have clustered tightly between $4.16 and $4.72, a range of just 13%. That is a far more realistic planning figure than the headline 2022 number. At a share price near $56, that range implies a yield of roughly 7.4% to 8.4% in normal conditions.

Why JEPI's Dividend Changes Every Month

JEPI's payout is variable by design. Understanding why prevents unnecessary panic when it drops.

JEPI runs two sleeves. The first is an actively managed portfolio of S&P 500 stocks selected through bottom-up fundamental research for low volatility and value characteristics — the prospectus commits at least 80% of assets to equity securities. This sleeve looks nothing like a market-cap-weighted index fund; positions are deliberately kept small and diversified.

The second sleeve is the income engine. Rather than writing calls directly against its own holdings, JEPI buys equity-linked notes (ELNs) — structured notes issued by large banks that combine S&P 500 exposure with written call options in a single instrument. The prospectus permits up to 20% of net assets in ELNs; in practice the allocation typically runs around 15%.

Option premiums scale with implied volatility. When markets get choppy, calls sell for more and JEPI's payout rises. When markets are calm and grinding upward, premiums shrink and the payout falls. This is why the distribution swings between roughly $0.26 and $0.62 rather than holding steady.

A falling JEPI distribution is not a dividend cut in the way a company reducing its payout is. It reflects a lower-volatility month. The trade-off runs the other way too: the record 2022 payouts came out of a bear market.

💰 How JEPI's Distributions Are Taxed

This is the detail most JEPI holders learn about in April rather than in advance. Because the majority of JEPI's income arrives through equity-linked notes, most of the monthly distribution is classified as ordinary income — taxed at your marginal rate — rather than as qualified dividends taxed at the preferential 15–20% long-term rate.

Only the slice attributable to dividends from the underlying stock holdings is generally qualified. The practical effect is that JEPI's headline yield and its after-tax yield can diverge meaningfully for investors in higher brackets, which is why the fund is frequently held inside IRAs and other tax-advantaged accounts.

Your actual tax treatment depends on your own situation and the year's final distribution classification, which JPMorgan reports on Form 1099-DIV. This page is not tax advice — consult a tax professional.

💡 How to Qualify for a JEPI Distribution

  • Buy before the ex-dividend date. Purchase no later than the prior trading day. Buying on the ex-date itself means you miss that month.
  • Ex-date and record date are the same day for JEPI, so there is no separate window to track.
  • You can sell on or after the ex-date and still receive the payment. Ownership before the ex-date is what matters.
  • The pay date lands about two business days later — faster than most monthly ETFs, which often run 5+ days.
  • Watch late December. The year-end ex-date is the one people miss. It is not in January.
