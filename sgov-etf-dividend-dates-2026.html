What $SGOV Is and Why People Ask About Its Dates

$SGOV is the iShares 0-3 Month Treasury Bond ETF. It launched on May 26, 2020 and owns U.S. Treasury bills that mature in three months or less. Its weighted average maturity is just 0.12 years, about six weeks. It has grown into a monster, with $119.0 billion in net assets as of November 19, 2026, and it charges 0.09%, or $9 a year on $10,000.

People don't buy $SGOV to get rich. They buy it as a place to park cash: an emergency fund, money waiting for a dip, a house down payment, or the "dry powder" part of an income portfolio. The share price barely moves, and the T-bill interest gets paid out every month.

Here's the part most people miss: because the price is so stable, the dividend calendar is the whole game with $SGOV. The price creeps up a few cents as interest builds during the month, then drops by about the payout on the ex-date. If you buy the day before the ex-date and sell right after, you haven't gained anything. You just moved the interest you already owned into your cash balance.

Why doesn't $SGOV pay a dividend in January?

It does, just early. iShares says its monthly bond and equity ETFs go ex-dividend on the first business day of each month except January, which is recognized in mid-December. That's why you see two December payouts and no January ex-date. In 2025, $SGOV went ex on December 1 ($0.312906) and again on December 19 ($0.323046), then skipped straight to February 2, 2026. Twelve payouts a year, the calendar just bunches two of them into December. If you build a monthly budget off $SGOV, plan for that January gap.

How $SGOV Dividends Are Taxed

Not qualified dividends. $SGOV's payouts come from Treasury bill interest, so they're taxed as ordinary income at your regular federal rate, not the lower qualified dividend rate. That's one of the most searched questions about this fund, and the answer surprises people.

Often state tax friendly. Interest from U.S. Treasury securities is generally exempt from state and local income tax, and because $SGOV owns Treasuries, a large share of its income may qualify for that exemption in many states. Rules vary by state (some only allow it if a fund holds enough Treasuries), and iShares publishes the U.S. government obligation percentage each tax season. Check it against your state's rules or ask a tax pro.

Where I'd hold it: if you live in a high income tax state, the state exemption is a real edge for $SGOV in a taxable account versus a bank savings account. In an IRA, none of this matters.

The Risks I'd Want You to Know

  • Rate risk to your income. The yearly payout went from about $0.03 a share in 2021 to over $5 in 2024 and back down to about $4.12 in 2025. Rate cuts hit $SGOV's check fast.
  • Inflation. After taxes and inflation, T-bills often leave you roughly even. It protects money, it doesn't grow it.
  • Opportunity cost. Cash parked for years in $SGOV has historically lagged stocks by a lot. Fine for a cushion, expensive as a long-term plan.
  • Buying right before the ex-date doesn't help. The price drops by about the payout. There's no free lunch in "dividend capture" here.
  • It's still a fund, not an FDIC account. The holdings are backed by the U.S. government, but the ETF itself is not bank insurance.

Frequently Asked Questions: SGOV Dividends

  • When is SGOV's next dividend?+
    $SGOV's next ex-dividend and record date is Monday, December 1, 2026, with payment on Friday, December 4, 2026. A second December distribution goes ex on December 18 and pays December 23, 2026, per the iShares 2026-2028 distribution schedule. There is no January ex-date.
  • Does SGOV pay dividends monthly?+
    Yes. $SGOV normally goes ex-dividend on the first business day of each month and pays about three business days later. The January payment is moved into mid-December, so you get two December payouts and none in January, still 12 a year.
  • How much does SGOV pay per share?+

  • Are SGOV dividends qualified?+
    No. $SGOV's distributions come from U.S. Treasury bill interest and are generally taxed as ordinary income, not as qualified dividends. Treasury interest is generally exempt from state and local income tax, so part or all of $SGOV's income may be state tax exempt depending on your state's rules. Check with a tax professional.
  • Why is there no SGOV dividend in January?+
    iShares recognizes the January distribution for its monthly bond ETFs in mid-December. In 2025 $SGOV went ex on December 1 and December 19 and then on February 2, 2026. For 2026 the December dates are December 1 and December 18.
