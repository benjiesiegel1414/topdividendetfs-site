Why People Search for JPMorgan's Highest Yielding ETFs

JPMorgan basically created the modern covered call income ETF boom. $JEPI and $JEPQ are two of the biggest income ETFs on the planet, with roughly $46 billion and $44 billion in assets. So when people want a high yield from a name they trust, they type "JPMorgan highest yielding ETF" into Google.

Here's the thing though. JPMorgan doesn't publish one neat "highest yield" list, and its lineup has changed. In March 2026 it added two new funds, $ROCY and $ROCQ, that work differently from $JEPI and $JEPQ.

So I put the whole list in one place, ranked by yield, with the numbers I actually look at: yield, price decay, fees and total return. You can always see the live version on our JPMorgan ETF page, which updates daily.

The short version

If you only want the biggest JPMorgan check, it's $JEPQ. If you want something calmer, it's $JEPI. $JPIE is a bond fund, not a stock fund, so it belongs in a different bucket. $ROCY and $ROCQ are the new kids, built to keep more upside, and they pay a smaller yield for it.

$JEPQ owns Nasdaq-100 style growth stocks and generates extra income by selling call options on the Nasdaq-100, mostly through equity-linked notes. Because tech is more volatile than the broad market, those options pay bigger premiums, and that's why $JEPQ yields more than $JEPI.

The latest payout was $0.56687 per share (ex-date October 1, 2026). Over the last 12 months it paid about $6.88 per share, and the August 2026 check of $0.70497 was its biggest of the year. Including dividends, it returned about 13.7% over the past year, and our list shows no price decay.

The catch: it rides tech. When the Nasdaq sells off, $JEPQ goes down with it. The income cushions the drop, it doesn't stop it. I keep every ex-date on the $JEPQ 2026 dividend dates page and the full record on $JEPQ dividend history.

$JEPI is the original. It holds a defensive, lower-volatility basket of S&P 500 stocks picked by JPMorgan's research team, then sells S&P 500 call options through equity-linked notes to create the monthly income.

The October 2026 payout was $0.34134 per share and the trailing 12 months add up to about $4.56. Its 1-year total return of about 7.6% trails $JEPQ, which makes sense: lower volatility stocks, smaller option premiums, smaller swings both ways.

This is the one I'd point a more conservative income investor toward if they want JPMorgan. Dates are on the $JEPI 2026 dividend calendar.

3. $JPIE: JPMorgan's Monthly Bond Income ETF

$JPIE (JPMorgan Income ETF) is an actively managed multisector bond fund. It launched October 28, 2021, has about $11.2B in assets, charges 0.39%, and yields around 6%. The latest monthly payout was $0.21589 per share (October 1, 2026).

Two things to know. First, it's bonds, so it behaves nothing like $JEPI or $JEPQ. Second, our list flags price decay on it, and its 1-year total return was only about 2.4% even with a 6% yield. That tells you the share price slipped. For a bond fund that's not a disaster, but it's not a growth engine either.

4 and 5. $ROCY and $ROCQ: The New Equity Premium Yield ETFs

J.P. Morgan launched $ROCY (JPMorgan Equity Premium Yield ETF) and $ROCQ (JPMorgan Nasdaq Equity Premium Yield ETF) on Nasdaq in March 2026. Both charge 35 basis points and pay monthly.

The difference from $JEPI and $JEPQ is how they handle the options. Instead of selling plain calls, they sell call spreads, which brings in less premium but lets the fund participate more when stocks rip higher. JPMorgan also says the funds seek return-of-capital distributions, which can be more tax-friendly in a taxable account. $ROCY focuses on U.S. large-cap core stocks, $ROCQ on Nasdaq stocks.

$ROCY has about $847M in assets and a ~5.8% yield on our list. $ROCQ paid $0.49122 per share in October 2026. They're interesting, but with about seven months of history I wouldn't judge them yet.

JPMorgan vs the Other Big Income ETFs

This is the part most people miss. JPMorgan is not the highest yielding issuer out there, not even close. Single-stock option funds on our best high dividend yield ETFs of 2026 list pay 30%, 50%, even higher. But most of them have serious price decay.

JPMorgan's edge is size, low fees and no decay on $JEPI and $JEPQ. Against the other Nasdaq-100 income funds, $JEPQ sits in the middle on yield. $GPIQ from Goldman Sachs pays a bit less but has been strong on total return, and I broke that down on the $GPIQ dividend dates page. If total return is your main yardstick, ETFTotalReturns.com ranks income ETFs by it.

The Risks I'd Want You to Know

  • Capped upside. Selling calls trades away part of the upside for income. In a big bull run, $JEPI and $JEPQ will lag the S&P 500 and Nasdaq-100.
  • Variable payouts. The monthly check moves with option premiums. $JEPQ paid $0.70497 in August 2026 and $0.56687 in October. Budget off the yearly total.
  • Taxes. Most of the $JEPI and $JEPQ income comes from the option overlay and is generally taxed as ordinary income, so many people hold them in an IRA. Check your 1099 and talk to a tax pro.
  • Bond risk on $JPIE. Rates and credit spreads drive it, not the stock market.
  • Short history on $ROCY and $ROCQ. Under a year of data.

My Takeaway

If you're searching for JPMorgan's highest yielding ETF, the answer is $JEPQ at roughly 11.6%, paid monthly, with no price decay on our list and a solid 13.7% total return over the past year. $JEPI is the steadier sibling at about 8%.

Here's how I think about it. I don't buy an income ETF because of the yield number alone. I want three things: reliable monthly payouts, no price decay, and a total return I can live with. $JEPQ and $JEPI both check those boxes right now. $JPIE is a bond fund with some decay, and $ROCY and $ROCQ need more time before I'd trust the numbers.

And remember, an 11% yield that keeps your principal intact beats a 40% yield that slowly melts. That's the mindset that actually builds income over time.
