Same Playbook, Different Index

$SPYI is the NEOS S&P 500 High Income ETF, with an inception date of August 29, 2022. $QQQI is the NEOS Nasdaq-100 High Income ETF, which came later, with an inception date of January 29, 2024. Together they hold about $27 billion: $14.8 billion in $QQQI and $12.6 billion in $SPYI as of November 10, 2026.

The strategy is the same in both. The fund owns the stocks in its index, then sells call options on the index itself (SPX for $SPYI, NDX for $QQQI) to generate monthly income. NEOS can also buy calls, which lets the fund keep some upside instead of capping all of it like an old school covered call fund. Both use index options that qualify as Section 1256 contracts, taxed 60% long term and 40% short term, and NEOS says both funds' distributions have been classified as return of capital.

Here's the part most people miss: the yield difference isn't magic. The Nasdaq-100 is more volatile than the S&P 500, and more volatility means fatter option premiums. $QQQI pays more because it's taking more risk. That's the whole trade.

The top four are the same in both funds, just a little heavier in $QQQI. The difference shows up after that. $SPYI's top 10 includes a drugmaker (Eli Lilly), and the rest of the S&P 500 brings in banks, energy, healthcare and industrials. $QQQI is 100 Nasdaq names, so it leans hard into tech, chips and growth, with no banks and very little energy. If you already own a lot of tech, $QQQI doubles down on it. If you want an income fund that looks more like the whole market, that's $SPYI.

Which One I'd Rather Own

  • Pick $SPYI if you want the steadier ride, broader diversification and a share price that has held up since 2022. It's the one I'd make the core of an income portfolio.
  • Pick $QQQI if you want the most income per dollar and you're comfortable with Nasdaq-100 swings. It pays about 3 points more, and in good tech years it can win on total return too.
  • Own both if you can't decide. Same fee, same pay date, same tax treatment. A 50/50 split lands around a 13.5% yield with less tech concentration than $QQQI alone.

Personally, I'd rather own a fund that pays 12% and holds its price than chase 15% and ride every tech selloff. But that's a preference, not a rule. When I was building toward financial freedom, the funds I leaned on were the ones I could hold through a bad month without wanting to sell. Know which one that is for you. The Risks I'd Want You to Know

  • Both lag their index over time. Selling calls trades upside for income. $SPYI returned about 14.3% a year since inception versus about 18.0% for the S&P 500 (NEOS, October 31, 2026).
  • $QQQI is a tech bet. Concentrated in a handful of mega caps and chip stocks. When the Nasdaq-100 drops, it drops with it, like the 5.91% hit in October 2026.
  • Neither payout is fixed. Both have been steady, but they depend on option premiums and volatility, and they can be cut.
  • Return of capital cuts both ways. It defers tax and lowers your cost basis, but you'll owe more in capital gains when you sell. Read the 19a-1 notices and your 1099. I explained it in why NEOS ETFs are tax friendly.
  • 0.68% isn't cheap. That's $68 a year on $10,000 in either fund, versus a few dollars for a plain index fund.

My Takeaway

$QQQI pays more, about 15% versus 12%, and $SPYI holds its price better. Since $QQQI launched, $10,000 in either one ended up within about $150 of the other with the cash taken out. They share the same fee, the same strategy, the same tax setup and the same pay dates. The only real choice is S&P 500 or Nasdaq-100.

If you want one fund to anchor your monthly income, I lean $SPYI for the diversification. If you want to squeeze more income out of the same dollars and you can handle tech volatility, $QQQI earns its spot. Either way, track the total return, not just the yield. Frequently Asked Questions: SPYI vs QQQI

  • Is $SPYI or $QQQI better?+
    It depends on what you want. $QQQI pays more, about 15% over the last 12 months versus about 12% for $SPYI, and it tracks the more tech heavy Nasdaq-100. $SPYI is more diversified across the S&P 500 and held its share price better. From QQQI's first close on January 30, 2024 through November 10, 2026, $10,000 with the cash taken out ended up almost even: about $14,310 in $QQQI and about $14,150 in $SPYI.
  • Which pays more, $SPYI or $QQQI?+
    $QQQI. Its last 12 monthly payouts total $7.6309 per share, about 15.0% of its $50.78 price on November 10, 2026. $SPYI's last 12 total $6.3222 per share, about 12.1% of its $52.21 price. On $10,000 that is roughly $1,500 a year from $QQQI versus $1,210 from $SPYI, before taxes.
  • Do $SPYI and $QQQI pay on the same day?+
    Yes. NEOS runs both on the same monthly calendar. The next distribution for both goes ex on November 18, 2026 and pays on November 20, and December goes ex on December 16 and pays on December 18. The amounts are announced the day before the ex-date.
  • Does $QQQI have price decay?+
    Not on our list. $QQQI closed its first day at about $49.84 on January 30, 2024 and closed at $50.78 on November 10, 2026, after paying $20.53 per share in distributions. Its price did fall from a $57.69 high in June 2026 as the Nasdaq-100 pulled back, so it moves with tech stocks.
  • Can you own both $SPYI and $QQQI?+
    Yes, and plenty of income investors do. They share the same strategy, fee (0.68%) and pay dates, but different indexes. Splitting between them gives you a blended yield of roughly 13% to 14% with a bit less tech concentration than $QQQI alone. Overlap is high because the largest Nasdaq-100 stocks are also the largest S&P 500 stocks.
