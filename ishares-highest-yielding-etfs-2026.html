Which iShares ETF Should You Pick?

If you want option income from BlackRock, $BALI is the one.

If you want cash-like income, $SGOV holds T-bills and pays monthly.

If you want long-term dividend growth with tiny fees, $DGRO, $HDV and $DIVB charge 0.08% or less. $DVY and $IDV yield more but cost more, and $IDV shows price decay on our list.

This is educational, not a recommendation. The right fund depends on your goals, your taxes and how much volatility you can stomach.

The Risks I'd Want You to Know

  • Price decay. Our list flags price decay on $IDV. A big yield doesn't help if the share price keeps sliding.
  • Bond and T-bill funds behave differently. $SGOV is a bond / T-bill fund, driven by interest rates and credit, not the stock market.
  • Lower yields. Most iShares dividend funds yield under 4%, so they build income slowly.
  • Variable payouts. Option income funds pay different amounts each period. Budget off the yearly total, not one big check.
  • Taxes. Option income is often taxed as ordinary income, and some payouts are return of capital. Check your 1099 and talk to a tax pro.

My Takeaway

If you're searching for iShares's highest yielding ETF, the answer is $BALI at roughly 6.2%, paid monthly, with no price decay on our list. The best total return since launch in the group belongs to $DVY at +617%.

Here's how I think about it. I don't buy an income ETF because of the yield number alone. I want three things: reliable payouts, no price decay, and a total return I can live with. A 10% yield that keeps your principal intact beats a 40% yield that slowly melts.
