What $MSTY Actually Is

$MSTY is the YieldMax MSTR Option Income Strategy ETF. It launched on February 21, 2024 and it's built around one stock: Strategy Inc ($MSTR), the company formerly known as MicroStrategy that holds a huge pile of bitcoin.

Here's the part most people miss. $MSTY doesn't own $MSTR shares. It holds mostly Treasury bills, notes and cash, gets its $MSTR exposure through options, and sells call spreads on $MSTR to harvest premium. As of November 2, 2026, about a third of the fund was cash and most of the rest was Treasuries, with $MSTR call options on top. $MSTR is one of the most volatile large caps in the market, so the option premium is huge. That premium is where the giant yield comes from.

The fund manages about $959 million, charges a 1.03% gross expense ratio, and went from paying monthly to paying weekly in October 2025. If you want the same kind of breakdown for a diversified YieldMax fund, I did the $CHPY dividend history too.

When is the next $MSTY dividend?

If the normal pattern holds, the next $MSTY payout gets declared Wednesday, November 4, 2026, goes ex on Thursday, November 5, and pays Friday, November 6. The amount isn't known until it's declared. Watch Thanksgiving week: in 2025 the payout went ex on Friday, November 28 because Thursday was the holiday.

To get a week's check you need to own $MSTY before the market opens on the ex-date. Buying on the ex-date itself is too late for that week.

Why older $MSTY payouts look so much bigger

$MSTY did a 1-for-5 reverse split after the close on December 5, 2025, and started trading split-adjusted on December 8, 2025, per the fund's SEC filing. Every 5 shares became 1 share worth about 5 times as much. YieldMax's distribution table shows the payouts before that date as they were originally paid, not split-adjusted. So the $0.1388 paid in early December 2025 is roughly $0.69 in today's shares, and the $4.13 checks from 2024 were paid on pre-split shares, each worth about a fifth of a share today. Don't compare old and new amounts without adjusting for the split.

The MSTY Dividend Yield: 60% Is Not What You Think

YieldMax lists a 60.23% distribution rate as of October 28, 2026. That number just takes the newest weekly check, multiplies it by 52 and divides by NAV. Our live list shows about 60% too. Meanwhile the 30-day SEC yield, which only counts actual net investment income and leaves out option income, was 0.96%.

And on a 2026 basis it's even wilder: $13.38 per share paid in about ten months against a $12.86 share price. A yield north of 100%.

Here's what I actually look at instead. YieldMax estimates that 25 of the 44 payouts in 2026 were 90% or more return of capital. Return of capital isn't automatically bad (it lowers your cost basis instead of being taxed right away), but when it's this consistent it usually means a big part of the "income" is your own money coming back to you. The 1099 at year-end settles the real tax character, so check the 19a-1 notices and your tax forms.

The Risks I'd Want You to Know

  • One stock, and a wild one. $MSTY lives and dies with $MSTR, which moves with bitcoin. YieldMax's own disclosure: the fund is exposed to all of $MSTR's downside but only part of its upside.
  • Capped upside. Selling calls means big rallies get partly sold away. The fund keeps the losses and gives up some of the gains, which is why the price tends to grind lower over time.
  • Heavy return of capital. Most 2026 payouts are estimated to be mostly ROC. It can be tax friendly in the short run, but it can also mean you're being handed back your own principal.
  • Shrinking checks. When the share price drops or volatility cools, the payout drops. The second half of 2026 has paid roughly 40% less per week than the first five months.
  • Not a bond, not a paycheck. The weekly amount changes every single week. Never build fixed expenses around it.

My Takeaway

$MSTY pays weekly: declared Wednesday, ex-date Thursday, paid Friday. It has paid $13.38 per share across 44 checks in 2026, and the latest was $0.1630. The 60% distribution rate is real cash, but a lot of it is estimated return of capital, and the one-year total return was about -47% through September.

I'd treat $MSTY as a leveraged-feeling bet on $MSTR and bitcoin that happens to spin off cash, not as an income foundation. If you own it, size it small, reinvest or redeploy the checks on purpose, and track total return every quarter. I'd rather build the core of an income portfolio on funds with no price decay and keep something like this as a satellite, if at all.
